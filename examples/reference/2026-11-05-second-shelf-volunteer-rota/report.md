## Shortlist

### 1. Neighbour shifts for parcel households

**Card:** ideas/provocation.md › 1. Neighbour shifts for parcel households
**Source:** Provocation, Challenge, Reversal, Concept Fan, Fractionation, SCAMPER, Six Hats (converged)
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and the furthest from the baseline: volunteers come from the households served rather than from publicity; seven techniques reached it independently.
**Movement:** Positive aspects: if recipients ran distribution, the workforce would be the people who know the service best and turn up every Saturday. The food bank can keep that benefit by opening a voluntary route from receiving to helping, without handing anyone the whole operation. / Challenge: Dropped the split between giver and receiver. The 220 households every Saturday are the biggest pool of adults with a direct tie to the food bank, already standing at the door, and the food bank had never counted them as possible volunteers. / Reversal: Originally help flows one way, from volunteers to the 220 households. Sending it the other way shows that the people already in the queue every Saturday are a large, younger group nobody asks. Some of them would welcome the chance to be on the other side of the table. / Concept Fan: Purpose → direction "grow volunteers from inside the existing community" → the concept flips the 220 households from clients to a recruitment pool already present each Saturday → an opt-in crew invitation at the distribution line. / Fractionation: Detaching the households from their role as recipients and joining them to the idle waiting hour turned dead time into the briefing slot, and the people already on site into the recruitment pool. / SCAMPER: The flip was on "the role of the household", turning a receiver into a provider. That opens a pool of hundreds of adults, many of them younger, who already come every Saturday and know how the distribution works from the queue side. / Six Hats: White: about 220 households come every Saturday and the food bank already has their attention at the right time; Red: recipients may feel ashamed or obliged, so make the offer anonymous in look and fully optional; Black: any hint of conditionality would damage trust, so keep the ask separate from the parcel and say so explicitly; Yellow: the value is a ready pool of people who know the operation from the other side, so put welcome and language roles at the centre → a service population became a volunteer pool that is already on site on Saturdays.
**First step:** Put a no-pressure invitation, unlinked to parcels, in next Saturday's parcels for one easy role (queue welcome), with the date of a group briefing.

### 2. Group lift bookings

**Card:** ideas/random-entry.md › 7. Group lift bookings
**Source:** Random Entry, Provocation, Challenge, Reversal, Concept Fan, Fractionation, Alternatives, SCAMPER, Six Hats (converged)
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and replaces individual recruitment with groups that fill a whole Saturday station; all nine techniques converged on it, the strongest signal of the session.
**Movement:** A chairlift carries people up in seats of four who arrive together; recruiting ready-made groups instead of single people moves younger crews in whole and makes their own social bond the reason they keep coming. / Provocation: Focus on the difference: with groups as the unit, the coordinator recruits four teams instead of forty people, and the group itself supplies the social pull. Letting groups adopt dates alongside the individual rota gets that benefit for real. / Challenge: Replaced the individual with an existing group as the unit of commitment. Groups already have a shared calendar, peer pressure to turn up and an age profile far younger than the current pool, so retention comes from the group's own ties, not from the food bank. / Reversal: Originally the rota is built one person at a time, and each person has to commit alone. Flipping the unit from person to group means one yes brings in eight people. Workplaces and clubs skew younger and already have rosters and a reason to do things together, so they bring in under-50s and their organisation runs the rota. / Concept Fan: Purpose (reliable hands) → direction "recruit groups instead of individuals" → the concept moves the unit of commitment from a person to an organisation that refills its own roster → a monthly adopted Saturday per organisation. / Fractionation: Cutting the Saturday hand-out by group rather than by person, and joining it to the coordinator's four hours, showed that the scarce resource is scheduling time; handing scheduling to the groups themselves lets the parish and other groups supply whole crews at once. / Alternatives: The fixed point is enough hands on every Saturday, not enough individuals. Moving the unit from a person to a group made the team, not the coordinator, responsible for turning up, and pointed toward groups made up mostly of working-age people. / SCAMPER: The element was "the individual volunteer who signs up alone". Swapping the person for a ready-made group brings in people who already meet each week, are mostly under 50, and keep each other turning up. It also lifts the scheduling load off the coordinator's four hours. / Six Hats: White: Saturday distribution is fixed and the warehouse work is physical, and club members are mostly under 50; Red: athletes respond to team challenge and rivalry more than to a charity appeal, so frame it as a fixture with a leaderboard; Black: one club pulling out would leave a hole, so rotate four clubs across the month and keep regulars on the counter; Yellow: the greatest value is a whole squad of under-50s arriving at once, so make the club the unit of recruitment → an individual volunteer ask became a recurring team fixture that recruits younger people in blocks.
**First step:** Ask two existing groups (a five-a-side team and a workplace team) this week to take one station on one Saturday next month.

### 3. Saturday crèche, run by the elders

**Card:** ideas/alternatives.md › 1. Saturday crèche, run by the elders
**Source:** Alternatives, SCAMPER, Six Hats (converged)
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and turns the ageing regulars into the enabler: they run a children's corner so parents under 50 can serve; reached by Alternatives, SCAMPER and Six Hats.
**Movement:** Asking what makes a parent's time worth giving moved the reward from thanks after the fact to removing the obstacle beforehand. The crèche then needed staff, and the older volunteers were the natural, willing match. / SCAMPER: The element was "the Saturday morning shift", which clashes with parents' childcare. Merging it with something from outside the situation, a children's activity session, removes the main reason young parents can't come. Putting older volunteers in the corner also keeps them involved in work that suits them better. / Six Hats: White: volunteers must be over 18 and distribution is locked to Saturday, when many 30–45-year-olds have children at home; Red: parents feel guilty leaving children for a charity morning, so make it a family outing; Black: children in a warehouse are a safety risk, so keep them at a separate table away from the food and van; Yellow: the value is unlocking an entire age group that cannot otherwise come, so build the shift around their constraint → a barrier for parents became the shift's main selling point.
**First step:** Ask the parish whether its hall can host a supervised children's corner on Saturday mornings, and ask three older regulars to run it.

### 4. Ski-school credentials

**Card:** ideas/random-entry.md › 3. Ski-school credentials
**Source:** Random Entry, Challenge, Reversal, Concept Fan, Fractionation, Alternatives, SCAMPER, Six Hats (converged)
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and changes what volunteers get, a CV-ready food-safety credential instead of thanks; eight techniques converged on it.
**Movement:** Ski instructors are mostly young people who come for a season because it gives them a qualification and experience; the property "seasonal work that builds a CV" moved to volunteering as a credential path rather than charity alone. / Challenge: Replaced gratitude with practical value as the reward. Retirees volunteer for purpose and company, but students, career-changers and the unemployed weigh time against what it gives them, and a reference or certificate costs the food bank almost nothing. / Reversal: Originally volunteers serve the food bank and get a thank-you once a year. Reversing who serves whom asks what the food bank can give its volunteers. Under-50s often need references and proof of skills, and Second Shelf can provide them for almost nothing, so giving them becomes the reason people join. / Concept Fan: Purpose → direction "recruit groups instead of individuals" → the concept looks for a need volunteers already have (a CV line, a reference) → the food-safety briefing, a constraint, becomes the hook, offered as a credential. / Fractionation: Detaching the briefing from its role as a cost the newcomer pays and attaching it to the followers as something they get turned a gate into a draw; the coordinator's hours go into one batch session a month rather than many one-to-ones. / Alternatives: The briefing has to happen anyway to keep food safe. Once it became a qualification worth having outside the food bank, the compulsory step turned into the reason under-50s sign up. / SCAMPER: The element was "the food-safety briefing", currently a barrier every newcomer has to clear. Used for a different purpose and audience, it becomes a reward that young people want for their careers. Some of the €3,000 budget covers certification costs, and volunteers are still not paid. / Six Hats: White: the brief already requires a food-safety briefing and runs real logistics weekly, which are checkable skills; Red: young job seekers feel volunteering is a sacrifice, so frame it as a credential they earn; Black: people may leave once they have the reference, so set the track at 12 Saturdays and invite graduates to become shift leads; Yellow: the greatest value is a credential with real market weight, so tie it to the supermarket partners who hire → unpaid labour became a career asset that attracts under-50s on their own terms.
**First step:** Price an accredited food-hygiene certificate for a group of ten and draft the flyer for students and job-seekers.

### 5. Regulars become shift captains

**Card:** ideas/six-hats.md › 1. Regulars become shift captains
**Source:** Six Hats
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and keeps the over-65 regulars by turning each into a seated captain who brings in and inducts newcomers, a different role rather than a different recruitment channel.
**Movement:** White: 30 of 45 regulars are over 65 and eight left last year, likely from physical strain; Red: older volunteers feel pushed out by a "we need young people" message, so make them the people who hand the work on; Black: captains may not have younger contacts, so let the coordinator pair contactless captains with sign-ups from other channels; Yellow: the value is retained experience plus warm introductions, so put mentorship at the centre → a demographic liability became a mentoring structure that both retains and recruits.
**First step:** Ask four regulars who find heavy shifts harder whether they would captain a station from a chair, each bringing one newcomer they know.

### 6. Turn up first, sign up later

**Card:** ideas/provocation.md › 2. Turn up first, sign up later
**Source:** Provocation, Reversal, Concept Fan, SCAMPER (converged)
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and removes the sign-up step altogether: brief on the spot, decide after the shift; reached by four techniques.
**Movement:** Moment to moment: walking through a Saturday where sign-up comes last shows that people drop off between showing interest and doing a first shift. Putting the shift first removes that step, and the question about commitment comes when a yes is most likely. / Reversal: Originally people commit to a rota first and find out what it's like afterwards, and that upfront promise puts off anyone with an unpredictable schedule. Putting the experience first lets the Saturday sell itself. By the time someone is asked to commit, they already know the team and the work. / Concept Fan: Purpose → direction "reshape the work to fit working-age lives" → the concept cuts the unit of commitment from a standing weekly shift to a single hour with no planning → a drop-in slot that works as a funnel into the rota. / SCAMPER: The element removed was "the sign-up and onboarding funnel between interest and first shift", where many would-be volunteers drop out. Without it, getting someone to come once is all the recruitment there is, and the experience on the day does the persuading.
**First step:** Train two regulars to give the food-safety briefing and run one Saturday when walk-ins over 18 are welcomed at 8:30.

### 7. Graded runs

**Card:** ideas/random-entry.md › 1. Graded runs
**Source:** Random Entry
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and gives newcomers a light green entry level with a visible path to lead roles, changing the shape of commitment rather than the call for volunteers.
**Movement:** Ski slopes are graded so beginners know exactly what they are getting into; the rota today is one undifferentiated "volunteer" commitment, which scares off busy under-50s, so grading the commitment gives them a safe first slope.
**First step:** Sort every rota task into green, blue and black on one sheet and pin it up in the warehouse.

### 8. Pedal parcels

**Card:** ideas/alternatives.md › 3. Pedal parcels
**Source:** Alternatives
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and opens a new kind of task (cargo-bike home delivery) that attracts a club of mostly younger riders.
**Movement:** The fixed point is getting parcels to households, not getting volunteers into a warehouse. Moving some of the work onto the road created a role that appeals to active, younger people who would never pack crates.

### 9. Season pass cohorts

**Card:** ideas/random-entry.md › 2. Season pass cohorts
**Source:** Random Entry
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and replaces open-ended volunteering with eight-Saturday cohorts briefed together, a commitment newcomers can say yes to.
**Movement:** A ski lift pass is bought for a defined season with a clear end date; a bounded commitment with an exit is easier to say yes to, and batching starts into cohorts fits the coordinator's four spare hours better than one-by-one onboarding.
**First step:** Announce one eight-Saturday season starting in the new year, capped at twelve places, with a single group briefing on day one.

### 10. Ninety-minute micro-slots

**Card:** ideas/provocation.md › 5. Ninety-minute micro-slots
**Source:** Provocation, Alternatives, Six Hats (converged)
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and lets people with jobs help in 90-minute slots; reached by three techniques, though it sits closer to the existing rota than the ideas above.
**Movement:** Focus on the difference: if sign-up came after the shift, the first commitment would cost almost nothing. Cutting the commitment into small pieces keeps that cost low while still giving the coordinator a plan. / Alternatives: Holding "enough hands per Saturday" as the fixed point let the commitment shrink from being a regular to booking a slot. Borrowing from gym booking made a short, flexible pledge feel normal to people with busy weekends. / Six Hats: White: the coordinator has four hours a week and the shift structure is fixed to Saturday, so the format must run itself; Red: younger people balk at open-ended commitments but book short classes easily; Black: short slots could leave gaps at handover, so overlap them by 15 minutes and let the coordinator see the grid days ahead; Yellow: the greatest value is lowering the first-step cost, so build the "try one slot" path as the front door → a fixed rota became a self-service grid that converts casual tryers into regulars.
**First step:** Split next Saturday into 90-minute slots on a shared sign-up sheet and link it from the Facebook page.

### Bring-a-grandchild generations shift

**Card:** ideas/alternatives.md › 2. Bring-a-grandchild generations shift
**Source:** Alternatives
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 4. Framed as "bring a grandchild", many of the people the 30 over-65 regulars bring will be teenagers, and the brief requires every volunteer to be over 18. Fix: Call it "bring a younger adult", say in the invitation that the guest must be 18 or over (an adult grandchild, child, nephew, niece or neighbour), and check ages at the sign-in desk before the briefing.

### University service-learning partner

**Card:** ideas/alternatives.md › 4. University service-learning partner
**Source:** Alternatives
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 3. Being a placement provider for social-work or nutrition modules usually means a named supervisor who supervises and assesses each student and fills in termly forms, often on weekdays. That is more than the coordinator's four spare hours a week, and it contradicts the card's claim that the idea costs no coordinator time. Fix: Aim only at volunteering-credit or service-learning modules where students log their own hours and the university does the assessing. The coordinator's only task is to countersign a Saturday sign-in sheet once a month, and the partnership starts with one cohort per term.

### Run to the Shelf

**Card:** ideas/concept-fan.md › 1. Run to the Shelf
**Source:** Concept Fan
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 2 breaks the €3,000 annual budget. A weekly breakfast for a club group costs, for example, 20 runners × €3 × 52 Saturdays ≈ €3,100. That is more than the whole year's budget and leaves nothing for briefings or anything else. Fix: Ask one of the two partner supermarkets to donate the breakfast items each Saturday as a sponsored "Run to the Shelf" breakfast. Then the budget pays only for occasional extras.

### The briefing is the first shift

**Card:** ideas/fractionation.md › 2. The briefing is the first shift
**Source:** Fractionation
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 2: volunteers must pass the food-safety briefing, and here the newcomer handles food before passing it, because the briefing is only "passed" at the end of the shift. Fix: Open the paired first shift with the short briefing itself, delivered by the trained regular and signed off before the newcomer touches open food. The newcomer spends those minutes on non-food tasks (boxes, labels, shelving), so the first visit is still a real shift and the constraint holds.

### Supermarket staff on the sorting shift

**Card:** ideas/provocation.md › 3. Supermarket staff on the sorting shift
**Source:** Provocation, Challenge, Fractionation, Six Hats (converged)
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 2. If the staff rotate from group to group, every new group needs its own food-safety briefing and someone to supervise it on a weekday afternoon. That cannot fit in the coordinator's four spare hours a week. Fix: Ask each supermarket for a small fixed team (the same three or four people on a regular weekly slot), so that each person is briefed only once. Have a trained regular volunteer lead the weekday sorting shift.

### Skis come in pairs

**Card:** ideas/random-entry.md › 5. Skis come in pairs
**Source:** Random Entry, Provocation, Challenge, Reversal, Concept Fan, Fractionation, SCAMPER (converged)
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 3. Recruiting around 30 under-50s is the problem itself: the current channels (Facebook page, parish, supermarkets) brought in two volunteers last year, and the coordinator's four hours a week cannot run a recruitment campaign that large. Fix: Have each over-65 regular find their own partner among younger family, neighbours or parish contacts, with the coordinator only running the briefing and confirming the match. Recruitment then runs through 30 personal networks and not through the coordinator.

### Après-shelf lunch

**Card:** ideas/random-entry.md › 6. Après-shelf lunch
**Source:** Random Entry
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 3. The brief does not say the warehouse has a kitchen, and cooking for groups usually needs kitchen facilities and food-safety registration beyond the volunteers' short briefing. Feeding volunteers from donations meant for 220 households may also break the supermarkets' terms or their trust. Fix: Hold the lunch in the partner parish's hall kitchen and pay for simple food from the budget (about €40 a week, roughly €2,000 a year), so donated food stays with households and no new kitchen is needed.
