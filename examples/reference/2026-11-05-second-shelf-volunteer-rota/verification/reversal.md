## ideas/reversal.md

### 1. The briefing goes on tour

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. Offices and colleges are full of under-50s, so bringing the briefing to them adds younger people to the rota, which is the gap between today's 45 mostly over-65 regulars and a stable 40 with a third under 50.
**Backward chain:**

1. Groups in offices, colleges and the parish hall have sat the food-safety briefing at lunchtime, hold "cleared to volunteer" cards, and are booking first Saturday shifts through a QR link.
2. Every attendee who gets a card is cleared to volunteer, including the college students.
3. Host offices, colleges and the parish have agreed to lunchtime slots, and the cards and a QR link to a shift-booking form are printed and set up for a few hundred euros at most, well inside the €3,000 budget.
4. A retired veteran volunteer, free on weekdays, has been trained by the coordinator to deliver the short briefing, and the coordinator books the talks within the four spare hours a week.
5. The coordinator asks the parish, which Second Shelf already has good relations with, to host the first session, and approaches one or two local employers and colleges. This can be done today.

**Broken link:** Step 2. Many college students are under 18, and handing out cards to a whole group breaks the rule that volunteers must be over 18.
**Possible fix:** Check age when people book or when the card is issued, and give "cleared to volunteer" cards only to attendees confirmed to be 18 or over. At colleges, offer the session only to adult or final-year students.

### 2. The newcomer runs the Facebook page

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. Clips made by young peers about real Saturdays reach under-50s in a way that recruitment calls don't, so the idea works on the under-50 share and the size of the rota.
**Backward chain:**

1. Each quarter the newest under-30 volunteer runs the Facebook page and posts short behind-the-scenes clips of Saturdays, with the veterans as the stars, and younger viewers sign up.
2. Every quarter there is a new volunteer under 30 who is willing to take over the page.
3. The veterans and any households that appear in the clips have agreed to be filmed under a simple consent and privacy rule, such as never showing a recipient's face without consent.
4. The coordinator has written a one-page posting guide and set up a handover of admin access, using a few of the four spare hours a week and no budget.
5. The coordinator hands the page to the youngest current volunteer. This can be done today.

**Broken link:** Step 2. Second Shelf recruited only two volunteers last year, so nothing in the current situation guarantees a new under-30 volunteer every quarter. The idea relies on the inflow it is meant to create.
**Possible fix:** Give the page to the youngest active volunteer, whatever their age, and keep the role for a set term rather than tying it to the latest under-30 arrival. Pass it to a new under-30 volunteer once one joins.
