## ideas/challenge.md

### 1. Self-run swap board with shift captains

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. Once it is running, Saturdays are covered by a pool of at least 40 people who each give as many Saturdays as suits them. That deals with both halves of the Problem: fewer people leave from burnout or guilt over absences, and people under 50 with irregular working and family weeks, who cannot promise a fixed weekly slot, can now join. The pool can grow to the Desired state's 40 active volunteers, a third of them under 50.
**Backward chain:**

1. A year on, at least 40 active volunteers, at least a third of them under 50, claim and swap Saturday shifts on a shared board. A rotating volunteer captain checks coverage each week, and the coordinator is no longer the rota's bottleneck.
2. Before that, younger people have joined because the offer is "give one Saturday a month, or whenever suits you". Each new joiner has passed the short food-safety briefing before the board lets them claim a shift, which keeps the over-18 and briefing rule.
3. Before that, the flexible offer has been advertised on the 1,200-follower Facebook page, in the parish newsletter and in the two partner supermarkets. Short group briefings run on a weekday at the warehouse, which is open Tuesday to Saturday, so new joiners can start without adding to the coordinator's Saturday load.
4. Before that, the existing 45 regulars use the board for a trial period of a few Saturdays. Volunteers who do not use it online have a paper copy at the warehouse, and the week's captain phones them, so the over-65 majority is not shut out.
5. Before that, a few trusted regulars have agreed to take turns as shift captain. Each has a one-page checklist: confirm coverage by Thursday, fill any gaps from the board's reserve list, and greet any newcomer.
6. Before that, the coordinator has set up a free or low-cost shift board (a shared sign-up tool or spreadsheet) with each Saturday's roles. They do it in their four spare hours a week, at little or no cost to the €3,000 budget. This is achievable today.
