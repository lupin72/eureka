### 3. Run it like parkrun

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** A public list of named roles with self-service slot-claiming and milestone tokens lowers the bar for working-age people who cannot give every Saturday, and gives everyone a visible reason to keep coming. That works on both recruitment and retention, so it moves the rota toward 40 active volunteers with a third under 50.
**Backward chain:**

1. At least 40 active volunteers, a third of them under 50, claim named Saturday roles online, and regulars collect 10/25/50-shift tokens.
2. The role list and slot-claim page are live and promoted on the Facebook page (1,200 followers), in the parish newsletter and in the two partner supermarkets. New claimers do a short group food-safety briefing before their first shift.
3. The coordinator has defined the Saturday roles, set up a free sign-up tool (a shared sheet or a free-tier sign-up service), scheduled a monthly group briefing, and ordered low-cost milestone tokens (well within €3,000; they are recognition, not pay).
4. The coordinator spends part of the four spare weekly hours on the setup. Achievable today with existing staff, the Facebook page and partner relationships.

### 4. The four-Saturdays pledge

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** A small, fixed pledge suits busy, younger people and can grow the pool past 40, with some pledgers becoming regulars. That addresses both the headcount and the under-50 share in the Desired state.
**Backward chain:**

1. The rota has at least 40 active volunteers, a third under 50, made up of remaining regulars plus a pool of pledgers each covering four chosen Saturdays, and some pledgers have become regulars.
2. Enough pledgers are signed up to fill the Saturday gaps. Each weekly regular lost has to be replaced by roughly ten to twelve pledgers, so the pool runs to dozens of people.
3. Every pledger has passed the food-safety briefing, and the coordinator has placed their four dates and keeps each Saturday evenly covered.
4. A pledge campaign runs on the Facebook page, in the parish newsletter and in the partner supermarkets, where people pick their four dates.
5. The coordinator writes the pledge offer and lists the year's Saturdays. Achievable today.

**Broken link:** Step 3. Individually briefing dozens of pledgers and hand-balancing their dates across 52 Saturdays goes beyond the coordinator's four spare hours a week.
**Possible fix:** Make the process self-serve. Pledgers book their four dates on an online sign-up sheet that caps the places on each Saturday, and new pledgers attend one fixed monthly group food-safety briefing, so the coordinator only publishes dates and runs one session a month.
