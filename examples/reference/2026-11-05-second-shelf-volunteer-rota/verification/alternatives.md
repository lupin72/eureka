### 3. Saturday crèche, run by the elders

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. A Saturday childcare corner removes the main barrier for parents under 50, the group the brief's Desired state needs, and moving older regulars into a lighter role cuts the losses that shrink the rota.
**Backward chain:**

1. Every Saturday a supervised children's corner runs at the warehouse, staffed in turns by over-65 regulars, and parents volunteer at the packing and handout stations while their children are there.
2. A small group of older regulars has agreed to take turns, has had the food-safety briefing plus a short safeguarding briefing, and knows the rule that a parent stays on site the whole time, so it is supervision and not registered childcare.
3. A corner of the warehouse away from the van bay and pallet area is fenced off and stocked with mats, books, colouring materials and a snack table, and the insurer has confirmed that the public liability cover includes children on the premises. This costs a few hundred euros of the €3,000.
4. The coordinator, in two or three weeks of spare hours, has written a one-page safeguarding and children's corner policy and asked older regulars at a Saturday handout which of them would rather read stories than lift crates.
5. Today: the coordinator rings the insurer and walks the warehouse to pick the corner. Both fit in this week's four spare hours.

### 4. Bring-a-grandchild generations shift

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. If quarterly generations Saturdays turn invited relatives and neighbours into regulars, the rota grows with younger people and older volunteers pass on what they know before they step back, which is the renewal the brief asks for.
**Backward chain:**

1. Each quarter a generations Saturday pairs older regulars with the younger people they invited, and a share of the newcomers sign up for the regular rota afterwards.
2. Newcomers who enjoyed the day are asked to join a regular slot before they leave, and the coordinator records who says yes.
3. Every invited newcomer passes the short food-safety briefing that morning, before going to a station.
4. Each of the 30 over-65 regulars has a younger relative or neighbour who is eligible to volunteer and has agreed to come.
5. The coordinator has announced the first generations Saturday and asked the 30 regulars to bring someone.
6. Today: the coordinator picks a date and drafts the invitation, using one week's spare hours.

**Broken link:** Step 4. Framed as "bring a grandchild", many of the people the 30 over-65 regulars bring will be teenagers, and the brief requires every volunteer to be over 18.
**Possible fix:** Call it "bring a younger adult", say in the invitation that the guest must be 18 or over (an adult grandchild, child, nephew, niece or neighbour), and check ages at the sign-in desk before the briefing.

### 6. Pedal parcels

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. A Saturday delivery role recruited through a cycling club brings in a block of active volunteers who are mostly under 50, which counts directly towards the 40 and the under-50 third.
**Backward chain:**

1. Every Saturday club riders and cargo-bike owners deliver parcels to housebound households along fixed routes, and the van is free for supermarket pickups.
2. Riders who are over 18 have had the food-safety briefing and carry insulated bags, so chilled items stay safe on the ride.
3. A short list of housebound households who want home delivery has their consent to share addresses with riders, grouped into a few fixed routes from the warehouse.
4. A local cycling club has agreed to adopt the deliveries as a regular Saturday club ride, and the insulated bags have been bought (a few hundred euros of the €3,000).
5. The coordinator has asked recipients at the Saturday handout who knows a housebound neighbour or relative, and has met the club captain.
6. Today: the coordinator finds a local cycling club and messages its captain, and puts a "do you know someone who can't get here?" note on the handout table. Both fit in this week's four spare hours.

### 7. University service-learning partner

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. A rolling cohort of students each term is a steady supply of adult volunteers under 30, and with some staying on after their module it can keep the rota at 40 with well over a third under 50.
**Backward chain:**

1. Each term a new group of students works a set number of Saturdays for course credit, and some carry on after the module ends.
2. The coordinator confirms each student's hours, and the university counts them towards the module.
3. Second Shelf is an approved placement provider: it has a signed placement agreement, a risk assessment, and a named supervisor who completes the university's placement paperwork and assessment forms for every student, every term.
4. A module leader at a nearby university or college has agreed that Saturday-only food-bank work meets the module's hour requirements.
5. Today: the coordinator emails the volunteering or service-learning office of the nearest university or college, within this week's spare hours.

**Broken link:** Step 3. Being a placement provider for social-work or nutrition modules usually means a named supervisor who supervises and assesses each student and fills in termly forms, often on weekdays. That is more than the coordinator's four spare hours a week, and it contradicts the card's claim that the idea costs no coordinator time.
**Possible fix:** Aim only at volunteering-credit or service-learning modules where students log their own hours and the university does the assessing. The coordinator's only task is to countersign a Saturday sign-in sheet once a month, and the partnership starts with one cohort per term.
