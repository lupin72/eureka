## ideas/provocation.md

### 1. Neighbour shifts for parcel households

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. The 220 households include working-age adults who are there every Saturday, so turning some of them into regular volunteers adds under-50s to the rota and helps close the gap between volunteers lost and volunteers recruited.
**Backward chain:**

1. A group of adults from parcel households hold regular Saturday or warehouse slots, and several of them are under 50.
2. Those who said yes are over 18, have passed the short food-safety briefing and have been given a regular slot.
3. Households have been invited in a way that is clearly opt-in: a note in the parcel and a word in the queue saying that a "neighbour shift" has no effect on their parcel.
4. The coordinator writes the invitation note and prints it for the next Saturday's parcels, which costs very little and fits in their four spare hours. This is achievable today.

### 2. Turn up first, sign up later

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. It turns first shifts into regular slots, which raises recruitment above two a year. Walk-ins brought in through the Facebook page skew younger than the current rota, so it moves Second Shelf towards both 40 volunteers and the under-50 share.
**Backward chain:**

1. Newcomers who worked a supervised first Saturday shift accept a regular slot, and the rota grows.
2. Each walk-in at 8:30 has an ID check to confirm they are over 18, passes the short food-safety briefing on the spot and is paired with an experienced regular.
3. A briefing script and a short quiz or checklist exist, and one or two regulars are trained to give them every Saturday, so the briefing does not depend on the coordinator.
4. The "just turn up at 8:30" offer is posted on the Facebook page (1,200 followers) and in the parish newsletter.
5. The coordinator writes the briefing script and the post and asks two regulars to act as buddies, within four spare hours and at no cost. This is achievable today.

### 5. Supermarket staff on the sorting shift

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Mostly. Supermarket staff are mostly working-age, so they add under-50 hands. Released staff do count as volunteers as long as the employer, not Second Shelf, pays for their time. The weekday sorting hours alone do not make a stable Saturday rota, though. Only the staff who move on to Saturdays, or a fixed weekday team, count towards the stable 40.
**Backward chain:**

1. Supermarket staff sort donations at the warehouse on weekday afternoons, and some have joined the Saturday rota in their own time.
2. Each staff member has passed the food-safety briefing and is supervised during the sorting shift.
3. Each supermarket releases groups of staff for warehouse shifts as employer-supported volunteering hours.
4. Store or regional managers at the two supermarkets have agreed to count those hours as volunteering or team-building time.
5. The coordinator asks the supermarket contacts they already know whether an employer-volunteering scheme exists. This is achievable today.

**Broken link:** Step 2. If the staff rotate from group to group, every new group needs its own food-safety briefing and someone to supervise it on a weekday afternoon. That cannot fit in the coordinator's four spare hours a week.
**Possible fix:** Ask each supermarket for a small fixed team (the same three or four people on a regular weekly slot), so that each person is briefed only once. Have a trained regular volunteer lead the weekday sorting shift.

### 6. After-work warehouse evenings

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. Evening slots open the rota to working people under 50 who cannot help during the day, and Saturday distribution stays as it is.
**Backward chain:**

1. One or two evening slots (18:00–20:00) run every week for sorting, packing and date-checking, staffed largely by people with day jobs.
2. Evening volunteers are over 18, have passed the food-safety briefing and have signed up for the evening slots.
3. Someone with keys and food-safety responsibility opens, supervises and locks the warehouse on each evening slot.
4. The evening slots are announced on the Facebook page and in the parish newsletter, with an evening briefing for new joiners.
5. The coordinator chooses which weekday tasks can move to evenings and picks the evenings. This is achievable today.

**Broken link:** Step 3. If the coordinator has to supervise two 2-hour evenings a week, that uses all four spare hours, which leaves nothing for briefings, recruitment or anything else.
**Possible fix:** Start with one evening a week, run by one or two trusted regular volunteers who are given keys and trained to supervise, so that it takes none of the coordinator's hours once it is set up.

### 7. Ninety-minute micro-slots

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. Single 90-minute slots lower the commitment that keeps busy younger people away, while regulars can still stack slots. That adds active volunteers, including under-50s, to a Saturday rota.
**Backward chain:**

1. The Saturday rota is filled in 90-minute slots. Newcomers book one at a time and regulars stack several.
2. Newcomers pass the food-safety briefing at the start of their first slot and confirm they are over 18 when they book.
3. A free shared sign-up sheet (for example, an online spreadsheet or form) lists the slots, is linked from the Facebook page and is announced to its 1,200 followers.
4. Each Saturday task has a clear handover at slot changes, so that a person who stays only 90 minutes is useful.
5. The coordinator splits the Saturday morning into slots and sets up the free sheet within four spare hours. This is achievable today.
