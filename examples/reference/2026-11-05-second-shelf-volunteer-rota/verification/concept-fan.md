### 7. Run to the Shelf

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. A running club that turns up as a group every Saturday adds a recurring block of mostly under-50 volunteers to the distribution rota. That moves Second Shelf towards 40 active volunteers with at least a third under 50, and the weekly ritual makes them stay, which is what keeps the rota stable.
**Backward chain:**

1. Every Saturday a local running club's social run finishes at the warehouse. The runners do a 90-minute distribution shift and then share a breakfast.
2. A breakfast for the whole group is supplied and paid for every Saturday of the year.
3. The participating runners are all over 18, have passed the food-safety briefing in one group session run by the coordinator, and are on the Saturday rota as a named group.
4. The club has agreed to make the shift part of its weekly Saturday run. It has routed the run to end at the warehouse and timed it to match distribution hours.
5. The coordinator has met a local running club with a written proposal, using part of their four spare hours a week. The parish or the Facebook page can supply the contact. This is achievable today.

**Broken link:** Step 2 breaks the €3,000 annual budget. A weekly breakfast for a club group costs, for example, 20 runners × €3 × 52 Saturdays ≈ €3,100. That is more than the whole year's budget and leaves nothing for briefings or anything else.
**Possible fix:** Ask one of the two partner supermarkets to donate the breakfast items each Saturday as a sponsored "Run to the Shelf" breakfast. Then the budget pays only for occasional extras.
