## ideas/random-entry.md

### 1. Graded runs

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** A one-slot-a-month green run with no lifting is a commitment working-age people can fit around jobs and families, so recruitment can outpace losses and bring in the under-50s the Desired state asks for. More small commitments still count as active volunteers on the rota.
**Backward chain:**

1. Second Shelf's rota runs on graded tasks: newcomers fill green slots, and some move up to blue and black by choice.
2. A first intake of newcomers has signed up for green runs, passed the food-safety briefing and been shadowed by existing blue or black volunteers.
3. The graded task list is published on the Facebook page, in the parish newsletter and on posters in the two partner supermarkets, with "two hours a month, no lifting" as the headline.
4. The coordinator has sorted every Saturday and warehouse task into green, blue and black and asked current regulars which grade they hold. This takes a few of the four spare hours and costs nothing, so it is achievable today.

### 2. Season pass cohorts

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** A free, bounded run of eight Saturdays is an easier yes for younger people than open-ended volunteering, and the renew-or-leave choice at each season end lets the rota be rebuilt four times a year towards 40 volunteers. Batching onboarding keeps the growth within the coordinator's time.
**Backward chain:**

1. Four cohorts a year start on set Saturdays. Each does the food-safety briefing together on day one, and at season end members renew, upgrade or leave with thanks.
2. The first cohort has signed up for a dated eight-Saturday season, and the coordinator has planned that season's rota around them.
3. The first season's start date and sign-up form are promoted on the Facebook page, in the parish newsletter and in the partner supermarkets.
4. The coordinator has picked the four start dates and set up a free sign-up form and a group briefing session. This fits the four spare hours and uses no budget, so it is achievable today.

### 3. Ski-school credentials

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** A recognised food-hygiene certificate, a reference letter and logged work hours are things job-seekers, students and career-changers need, almost all of them under 50. That gives them a reason to join and finish a full season, which feeds both the headcount and the under-50 share in the Desired state.
**Backward chain:**

1. Volunteers who complete a season leave with a recognised food-hygiene certificate, a reference letter and a log of their hours in warehouse logistics or van work.
2. The certificates are bought through an accredited online food-hygiene course at roughly €15–30 per person, so about 50 certificates use €750–1,500 of the €3,000 budget. A training cost is not payment, so it stays clear of "paying volunteers".
3. The coordinator has a reference-letter template and a simple hours log, so season-end paperwork takes minutes per volunteer and fits in the four spare hours.
4. The offer is pitched through the Facebook page and to local job centres, colleges and the parish, all reachable with existing channels and relationships.
5. The coordinator has chosen an accredited course provider and drafted the template. This is desk work within the four spare hours, so it is achievable today.

### 4. Bindings that release

**Verdict:** ❌ Not adherent
**Adherence:** With pauses and seated roles in place, fewer of the 30 over-65 regulars leave, so the rota may hold near 40. But nothing in the idea brings in under-50s, so the Desired state's "at least a third under 50" is missed and the ageing that causes the losses goes on.
**Backward chain:**

1. Any volunteer can pause for up to six months without leaving or redoing the briefing, and older regulars who can no longer lift work in seated roles.
2. Seated roles (intake desk, phoning households, stock lists) are defined and set up at the warehouse.
3. The coordinator keeps a pause register and a check-in reminder before each pause ends.
4. The coordinator has written down the release option and the seated roles and told the regulars. This needs no budget and fits the four spare hours, so it is achievable today.

**Actually solves:** Keeping existing, mostly older volunteers through illness, caring duties and declining strength, which slows the losses but does not renew the rota's age profile.

### 5. Skis come in pairs

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** If every over-65 regular hands their role to a younger partner, the rota keeps its know-how and changes its age profile as the older generation steps back, which delivers both the headcount and the under-50 share.
**Backward chain:**

1. Each over-65 regular works alongside a younger partner for a season, and when an older volunteer steps back, the partner takes over the role.
2. Around 30 younger partners have joined, passed the food-safety briefing, and committed to the same station and shift as their senior partner for a season.
3. Those 30 younger volunteers have been recruited.
4. The coordinator has matched each over-65 regular to a role they can pass on and listed the openings.

**Broken link:** Step 3. Recruiting around 30 under-50s is the problem itself: the current channels (Facebook page, parish, supermarkets) brought in two volunteers last year, and the coordinator's four hours a week cannot run a recruitment campaign that large.
**Possible fix:** Have each over-65 regular find their own partner among younger family, neighbours or parish contacts, with the coordinator only running the briefing and confirming the match. Recruitment then runs through 30 personal networks and not through the coordinator.

### 6. Après-shelf lunch

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** A weekly shared lunch makes the Saturday shift socially rewarding, which helps keep people, and the bring-a-guest rule recruits through personal networks, which tend to convert better than broadcast appeals. Together these address both sides of the losses-versus-recruits gap.
**Backward chain:**

1. Every Saturday distribution ends with a lunch for the volunteers who worked and one guest each, and some guests come back as volunteers.
2. A hot lunch for perhaps 20–40 people is cooked in the warehouse every Saturday from that day's surplus.
3. The warehouse has a kitchen that can legally prepare hot food for groups, and the supermarkets' donation terms allow surplus to feed volunteers and guests.
4. The coordinator has checked the warehouse facilities and the donation agreements.

**Broken link:** Step 3. The brief does not say the warehouse has a kitchen, and cooking for groups usually needs kitchen facilities and food-safety registration beyond the volunteers' short briefing. Feeding volunteers from donations meant for 220 households may also break the supermarkets' terms or their trust.
**Possible fix:** Hold the lunch in the partner parish's hall kitchen and pay for simple food from the budget (about €40 a week, roughly €2,000 a year), so donated food stays with households and no new kitchen is needed.

### 7. Group lift bookings

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Running clubs, five-a-side teams, parents' groups and workplace teams are mostly under 50 and arrive several people at a time, so a handful of groups can add the 20 or so volunteers needed and push the under-50 share past a third. One contact per group keeps the coordinator within four hours a week.
**Backward chain:**

1. Several local groups each staff one station on one Saturday a month as a crew and handle their own swaps.
2. Each crew member is over 18 and has passed the food-safety briefing, which the coordinator runs once per group.
3. Four or five groups have agreed to a monthly Saturday booking, each with one named contact.
4. The coordinator has pitched "book a lift" to local clubs, parents' groups and workplaces through the Facebook page, the parish and the staff of the two partner supermarkets.
5. The coordinator has written a one-page offer listing the stations groups can take over. This uses existing channels, costs nothing and fits the four spare hours, so it is achievable today.
