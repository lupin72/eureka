## ideas/six-hats.md

### 1. Regulars become shift captains

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. Giving over-65 regulars a lighter seated role slows the outflow, and making each captain bring in and mentor someone under 50 adds younger volunteers. Together these push toward a stable rota of 40 or more with at least a third under 50. If around 15 of the 30 older regulars become captains and each brings one person, that is roughly the 14 under-50 volunteers the target needs.
**Backward chain:**

1. Each Saturday, seated shift captains run the briefing, the parcel checklist and the welcome. Each has brought in and mentored at least one person under 50, and the rota holds at 40 or more active volunteers, a third of them under 50.
2. The newcomers the captains brought in are over 18, have passed the short food-safety briefing and are on the Saturday rota, each paired with a captain for their first month.
3. Each captain has asked people in their own network (neighbours, relatives, former colleagues) and at least one person under 50 has agreed to come on a Saturday.
4. The captains have been shown how to run the briefing, the checklist and the welcome. The coordinator does this inside the four spare hours a week, with a few euros of the €3,000 budget spent on printed checklists and a chair at each station.
5. A group of over-65 regulars has agreed to switch from heavy shifts to the captain role.
6. The coordinator has written a one-page description of the captain role and puts it to the regulars at the next Saturday distribution. This can be done today with existing people and time.
