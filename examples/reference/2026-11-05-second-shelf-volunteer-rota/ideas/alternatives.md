# Alternatives ideas

Elements:
- who recruits (fixed point: get new people to hear a personal, trusted invitation to help)
  - the coordinator; current volunteers asking friends; the parish priest
  - *second round:* each older volunteer brings a younger relative; employers' HR teams; a cycling or running club captain; past recipients who are back on their feet
- unit of commitment (fixed point: make sure every Saturday has enough hands)
  - individual weekly regulars; a monthly regular; an on-call reserve list
  - *second round:* a whole team adopts one Saturday a month; 90-minute micro-shifts booked like a gym class; a family signs up as one unit
- onboarding briefing (fixed point: make every newcomer safe to handle food)
  - a short talk at the warehouse; a printed leaflet; a video
  - *second round:* a recognised food-hygiene certificate that newcomers can put on a CV; a briefing delivered at a college or job centre; peer-taught on the first shift
- the thank-you (fixed point: make volunteers feel their time is worth giving again)
  - annual appreciation event; thank-you cards; a mention on Facebook
  - *second round:* a reference letter or skills record; a Saturday crèche so parents can come; a shared breakfast after every shift
- where and what the work is (fixed point: get parcels to the 220 households)
  - packing and handing out at the warehouse; a second packing evening; van driving
  - *second round:* Saturday bike deliveries to housebound households; older volunteers move to roles that suit them while younger ones do the lifting
- recruitment channel (fixed point: reach under-50s who would say yes if asked)
  - Facebook page; parish newsletter; supermarket posters; open day
  - *second round:* university service-learning modules; company volunteering-day schemes; job centre work-experience referrals

## 1. Saturday crèche, run by the elders

**Description:** Open a supervised children's corner at the warehouse on Saturdays so parents can volunteer without needing childcare. The over-65 regulars who find lifting crates harder take turns running it (story time, colouring, a snack table), which keeps them on the rota.
**Technique:** Alternatives, alternative: `the thank-you → a Saturday crèche so parents can come`; SCAMPER, operator: `Combine`; Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Make childcare the reason people can come, not the reason they can't`
**Movement:** Asking what makes a parent's time worth giving moved the reward from thanks after the fact to removing the obstacle beforehand. The crèche then needed staff, and the older volunteers were the natural, willing match. / SCAMPER: The element was "the Saturday morning shift", which clashes with parents' childcare. Merging it with something from outside the situation, a children's activity session, removes the main reason young parents can't come. Putting older volunteers in the corner also keeps them involved in work that suits them better. / Six Hats: White: volunteers must be over 18 and distribution is locked to Saturday, when many 30–45-year-olds have children at home; Red: parents feel guilty leaving children for a charity morning, so make it a family outing; Black: children in a warehouse are a safety risk, so keep them at a separate table away from the food and van; Yellow: the value is unlocking an entire age group that cannot otherwise come, so build the shift around their constraint → a barrier for parents became the shift's main selling point.
**Problem it solves:** It opens the door to parents under 50 whose Saturdays are taken up by their children, and it slows the loss of older volunteers by giving them a lighter role.
**Convergence:** Alternatives, SCAMPER, Six Hats

## 2. Bring-a-grandchild generations shift

**Description:** Ask each of the 30 over-65 regulars to bring one younger relative or neighbour to a "generations Saturday" each quarter, where the two work the same station side by side. The newcomer sees a familiar face on day one, and the older volunteer passes on what they know before stepping back.
**Technique:** Alternatives, alternative: `who recruits → each older volunteer brings a younger relative`
**Movement:** The fixed point was a trusted personal invitation. The most trusted people Second Shelf could ask to do the inviting are the very volunteers it is losing, and most of their families are under 50.
**Problem it solves:** It makes the ageing core of the rota the route younger people come in by, so the volunteer base renews itself instead of shrinking.

## 3. Pedal parcels

**Description:** Invite a local cycling club, and anyone with a cargo bike, to deliver Saturday parcels to housebound households along fixed routes. Riders sign up as a club activity, the ride itself is part of the reward, and the van is freed for supermarket pickups.
**Technique:** Alternatives, alternative: `where and what the work is → Saturday bike deliveries to housebound households`
**Movement:** The fixed point is getting parcels to households, not getting volunteers into a warehouse. Moving some of the work onto the road created a role that appeals to active, younger people who would never pack crates.
**Problem it solves:** It creates a new volunteer role that suits under-50s and brings in a whole club at once.

## 4. University service-learning partner

**Description:** Offer Second Shelf as a placement for a university or college service-learning, social-work or nutrition module: students do a set number of Saturdays for course credit and the coordinator signs off their hours. Each new class brings a fresh group, and some students stay on after the module ends.
**Technique:** Alternatives, alternative: `recruitment channel → university service-learning modules`
**Movement:** Looking for a channel where under-50s already need a reason to say yes led to courses that require community hours, so the university does the recruiting every term.
**Problem it solves:** It sets up a steady, self-renewing supply of adult volunteers under 30 without spending the coordinator's hours on campaigns.
