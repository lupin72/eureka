# Fractionation ideas

Fractions:

- The week (cut by moment in time)
  - Tuesday–Friday warehouse sorting
  - van supermarket run
  - Saturday parcel hand-out
- The people (cut by actor)
  - over-65 regulars' know-how
  - 220 households in the queue
  - supermarket staff
  - parish congregation
  - 1,200 Facebook followers
- The gates (cut by what each step costs the newcomer)
  - food-safety briefing
  - coordinator's four hours
- The feelings (cut by emotion)
  - the awkward first shift
  - the waiting hour in the queue
  - pride of a finished parcel
  - grief of a lost regular

## 1. Relay retirement

**Description:** No regular leaves alone: when a volunteer starts to step back, they spend their last months training a named successor they helped find, and their departure is marked as a handover, not a loss. Each relay pair works the same shifts for eight weeks, so the leaver's know-how travels with them into the next person, and the newcomer starts with a mentor instead of a stranger.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `grief of a lost regular + over-65 regulars' know-how + the awkward first shift`
**Movement:** Reordering the "grief of a lost regular" in time, so the exit comes before it happens rather than after, turned a departure into a recruiting moment; joining it to the leaver's know-how and the newcomer's awkward first shift made one person the answer to both.
**Problem it solves:** It ties every one of the departures that drain the rota to an arrival, so losses stop outpacing recruits.

## 2. Supermarket crew on the van run

**Description:** The two partner supermarkets put the van run into their staff volunteering scheme: one or two of their own employees ride the van on paid volunteering hours, load the donations they packed, and stay for a sorting shift. Supermarket staff already hold food-hygiene training, so the briefing shrinks to a quick top-up.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `supermarket staff + van supermarket run + food-safety briefing`
**Movement:** Detaching the van run from Second Shelf's own volunteer block and attaching it to the supermarket staff who already stand at the other end of it showed a ready pool of working-age people for whom the food-safety gate is already open.
**Problem it solves:** It brings in a steady flow of under-50 volunteers without spending the coordinator's hours on recruiting them one by one.
**Merged into:** ideas/provocation.md › 5. Supermarket staff on the sorting shift

## 3. Adopt-a-Saturday crews

**Description:** The rota is built from teams, not individuals: a parish group, a sports club, a workplace or a group of friends each adopts one Saturday a month and brings its own crew of six to eight. The coordinator schedules four team captains instead of forty names, and each captain fills and covers their own team's slots.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `Saturday parcel hand-out + parish congregation + coordinator's four hours`
**Movement:** Cutting the Saturday hand-out by group rather than by person, and joining it to the coordinator's four hours, showed that the scarce resource is scheduling time; handing scheduling to the groups themselves lets the parish and other groups supply whole crews at once.
**Problem it solves:** It grows the rota in blocks of several volunteers at a time, including younger members of the groups, within the coordinator's four spare hours.
**Merged into:** ideas/random-entry.md › 7. Group lift bookings

## 4. The queue as a doorway

**Description:** During the waiting hour on Saturdays, a volunteer offers adults in the queue, and the people who come with them, a quiet invitation to help with weekday sorting, with the food-safety briefing run right there at a table by the queue. People who once received parcels often want to give back, and many are under 50, but nobody has ever asked them.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `220 households in the queue + the waiting hour in the queue + food-safety briefing`
**Movement:** Detaching the households from their role as recipients and joining them to the idle waiting hour turned dead time into the briefing slot, and the people already on site into the recruitment pool.
**Problem it solves:** It taps a large local pool of working-age people who already know Second Shelf, without a recruitment budget.

## 5. Parcel count on the wall

**Description:** Each weekday sorting session posts its tally on the Facebook page that evening: "Tuesday crew packed 64 parcels," with a photo of the stacked shelves and the crew's first names. Followers can sign up for a single named session with one tap, so they join a crew with a visible result rather than an open-ended commitment.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `Tuesday–Friday warehouse sorting + pride of a finished parcel + 1,200 Facebook followers`
**Movement:** Joining the pride of a finished parcel, felt today only inside the warehouse, to the followers who never see it, turned the page from a noticeboard into a showcase of concrete results, and made the weekday shifts the way in.
**Problem it solves:** It turns passive followers into short-shift volunteers by showing them a finished piece of work they could be part of.

## 6. The briefing is the first shift

**Description:** The food-safety briefing stops being a step before volunteering and becomes a paired two-hour first shift: the newcomer sorts alongside a regular, who teaches the hygiene rules hands-on as they come up. Finishing the shift counts as passing the briefing, so a newcomer's first visit already makes them part of the rota.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `food-safety briefing + the awkward first shift + over-65 regulars' know-how`
**Movement:** Reordering the two gates so they happen at once, and joining them to the regulars' know-how, removed the extra visit where recruits drop out and replaced the awkward start with a working partner.
**Problem it solves:** It stops recruits dropping out between signing up and their first shift.

## 7. Briefing for the CV

**Description:** Second Shelf runs a monthly open food-safety session that comes with a certificate of attendance and, after ten shifts, a written reference for job seekers, students and people changing careers. The session draws working-age people for its own sake, and the reference rewards them for staying.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `food-safety briefing + 1,200 Facebook followers + coordinator's four hours`
**Movement:** Detaching the briefing from its role as a cost the newcomer pays and attaching it to the followers as something they get turned a gate into a draw; the coordinator's hours go into one batch session a month rather than many one-to-ones.
**Problem it solves:** It gives under-50s a concrete personal reason to join and stay, which brings the rota toward its target age mix.
