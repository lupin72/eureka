# Six Hats ideas

## 1. Sports club adopts a Saturday

**Description:** Local amateur sports clubs (running, rugby, rowing, five-a-side) each adopt one Saturday a month and staff the warehouse lifting and van loading as a team "away fixture", logged on a shared club leaderboard of kilos moved. The club captain does the food-safety briefing once with the coordinator and then cascades it to teammates before each fixture, so the coordinator's four hours go on relationships, not individual onboarding. A standing core of the current regulars runs the counter while the clubs handle the heavy work, keeping the rota stable even when one club drops a month.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Treat the Saturday shift as a team sport, not an individual commitment`
**Movement:** White: Saturday distribution is fixed and the warehouse work is physical, and club members are mostly under 50; Red: athletes respond to team challenge and rivalry more than to a charity appeal, so frame it as a fixture with a leaderboard; Black: one club pulling out would leave a hole, so rotate four clubs across the month and keep regulars on the counter; Yellow: the greatest value is a whole squad of under-50s arriving at once, so make the club the unit of recruitment → an individual volunteer ask became a recurring team fixture that recruits younger people in blocks.
**Problem it solves:** It brings in under-50 volunteers in groups rather than one at a time, replacing the loss of older regulars faster than individual recruitment can.

## 2. Regulars become shift captains

**Description:** Instead of losing over-65 volunteers when heavy shifts become too much, Second Shelf turns each of them into a seated "shift captain" who runs the briefing, the parcel checklist and the welcome for two or three newcomers. Each captain is responsible for bringing at least one person they know under 50 (a neighbour, a nephew, a former colleague) and mentoring them through their first month. The role is explicitly lighter on the body and heavier on knowledge, so the people most at risk of leaving get a reason to stay longer.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `The retiring volunteers are the recruitment channel, not the problem`
**Movement:** White: 30 of 45 regulars are over 65 and eight left last year, likely from physical strain; Red: older volunteers feel pushed out by a "we need young people" message, so make them the people who hand the work on; Black: captains may not have younger contacts, so let the coordinator pair contactless captains with sign-ups from other channels; Yellow: the value is retained experience plus warm introductions, so put mentorship at the centre → a demographic liability became a mentoring structure that both retains and recruits.
**Problem it solves:** It slows the outflow of older volunteers while using their networks to recruit younger ones, attacking both sides of the loss-versus-recruitment gap.

## 3. Volunteer hours as a work reference

**Description:** Second Shelf offers a structured "warehouse logistics" track for job seekers, students and career changers: after 12 Saturdays, the coordinator signs a reference describing concrete skills (stock rotation, food-safety compliance, van logistics for 220 households). The track is promoted through the local job centre, university career office and the supermarkets' own HR teams, who can recognise the reference. Part of the €3,000 covers a recognised food-hygiene certificate for those who complete the track.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Pay volunteers in references, not money`
**Movement:** White: the brief already requires a food-safety briefing and runs real logistics weekly, which are checkable skills; Red: young job seekers feel volunteering is a sacrifice, so frame it as a credential they earn; Black: people may leave once they have the reference, so set the track at 12 Saturdays and invite graduates to become shift leads; Yellow: the greatest value is a credential with real market weight, so tie it to the supermarket partners who hire → unpaid labour became a career asset that attracts under-50s on their own terms.
**Problem it solves:** It gives younger people a self-interested reason to commit for months, building the under-50 share of the rota.

## 4. Family shift with a kids' table

**Description:** One slot each Saturday is a "family shift": parents volunteer at packing and distribution while a separate kids' table, run by one volunteer with a children's reading or drawing activity tied to food, keeps under-18s occupied and supervised. Parents of young children, often the hardest age group to recruit because Saturdays are childcare days, can then volunteer without arranging a babysitter. The kids never handle food, so the over-18 rule holds.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Make childcare the reason people can come, not the reason they can't`
**Movement:** White: volunteers must be over 18 and distribution is locked to Saturday, when many 30–45-year-olds have children at home; Red: parents feel guilty leaving children for a charity morning, so make it a family outing; Black: children in a warehouse are a safety risk, so keep them at a separate table away from the food and van; Yellow: the value is unlocking an entire age group that cannot otherwise come, so build the shift around their constraint → a barrier for parents became the shift's main selling point.
**Problem it solves:** It opens volunteering to parents under 50 who are blocked by Saturday childcare, directly growing the younger share of the rota.

## 5. Recipients join the rota

**Description:** Households who receive parcels are invited, never required, to join the Saturday team, starting with roles that suit them, such as welcoming people in the queue in their own language or helping carry parcels. Each recipient-volunteer gets the same briefing and the same team T-shirt as everyone else, so no one can tell giver from receiver. Many recipients are younger and already present every Saturday, so the recruitment cost is almost nil; the open condition is that it must be framed carefully so nobody feels volunteering is the price of a parcel.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `The 220 households in the queue are already a Saturday crowd`
**Movement:** White: about 220 households come every Saturday and the food bank already has their attention at the right time; Red: recipients may feel ashamed or obliged, so make the offer anonymous in look and fully optional; Black: any hint of conditionality would damage trust, so keep the ask separate from the parcel and say so explicitly; Yellow: the value is a ready pool of people who know the operation from the other side, so put welcome and language roles at the centre → a service population became a volunteer pool that is already on site on Saturdays.
**Problem it solves:** It taps the one group reliably present at every distribution, many of them under 50, to fill the rota without a recruitment drive.

## 6. Bookable 90-minute micro-shifts

**Description:** The Saturday is cut into 90-minute slots (early unload, mid-morning packing, late distribution) that anyone can book like a gym class through a simple free booking page, with a reminder the night before. Newcomers can try one slot without committing to a whole morning, and repeat bookers are automatically invited to a "regular" status after six slots. The coordinator spends one of their four hours a week checking the next Saturday's grid and plugging gaps with regulars.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Sell the rota like fitness classes, by the slot`
**Movement:** White: the coordinator has four hours a week and the shift structure is fixed to Saturday, so the format must run itself; Red: younger people balk at open-ended commitments but book short classes easily; Black: short slots could leave gaps at handover, so overlap them by 15 minutes and let the coordinator see the grid days ahead; Yellow: the greatest value is lowering the first-step cost, so build the "try one slot" path as the front door → a fixed rota became a self-service grid that converts casual tryers into regulars.
**Problem it solves:** It lowers the commitment barrier that keeps busy under-50s from trying volunteering, feeding a steady pipeline into the rota.

## 7. Supermarket staff team days

**Description:** The two partner supermarkets each send a rotating pair of staff, on paid company volunteering time, to work the Saturday their own donations are distributed, following the food from their shelves to the households. Staff already hold food-hygiene training, so the briefing is a short top-up, and the supermarkets get a visible community story for their stores. Over time, staff who enjoy it are invited to join the rota in their own time.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Let the food bring its own volunteers with it`
**Movement:** White: Second Shelf already has good relations with two supermarkets and its Saturday schedule is built around their donations; Red: staff feel more ownership when they see where their store's food goes, so make it "follow your food"; Black: Saturday is a busy retail day, so ask for only two staff per store and let the stores pick the rota; Yellow: the value is a renewable source of food-trained, mostly working-age people, so make the donation partnership include people as well as food → a food supply relationship became a volunteer supply relationship.
**Problem it solves:** It adds a steady, renewable stream of trained working-age volunteers through partners Second Shelf already has.
