# Random Entry ideas

Random word: `ski`

## 1. Graded runs

**Description:** Every rota task gets a grade like a ski slope: green (one 2-hour Saturday slot a month, no lifting, shadowed), blue (fortnightly, a named station), black (weekly lead, van or warehouse key-holder). A newcomer signs up for a green run only, and moves up by choice, never by pressure.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `ski`
**Movement:** Ski slopes are graded so beginners know exactly what they are getting into; the rota today is one undifferentiated "volunteer" commitment, which scares off busy under-50s, so grading the commitment gives them a safe first slope.
**Problem it solves:** It lowers the entry cost that keeps working-age people from joining, so recruitment can outpace losses.

## 2. Season pass cohorts

**Description:** Instead of open-ended volunteering, Second Shelf sells free "season passes": a fixed run of eight Saturdays, starting four times a year, with everyone in the cohort doing the food-safety briefing together on day one. At the end of the season each person renews, upgrades or leaves with thanks, and the coordinator plans the rota a season at a time.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `ski`
**Movement:** A ski lift pass is bought for a defined season with a clear end date; a bounded commitment with an exit is easier to say yes to, and batching starts into cohorts fits the coordinator's four spare hours better than one-by-one onboarding.
**Problem it solves:** It turns vague, lifelong-sounding volunteering into a bounded yes that younger people will give, and batches onboarding so the coordinator can handle the volume.

## 3. Ski-school credentials

**Description:** Second Shelf becomes a place to earn something: volunteers who complete a season get a recognised food-hygiene certificate, a reference letter, and logged hours in warehouse logistics or van driving, paid from part of the €3,000 budget. It is pitched to job-seekers, students, career-changers and people needing references.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `ski`; Challenge, assumption challenged: `volunteers are kept by gratitude and goodwill`; Reversal, reversal applied: `the food bank serves the volunteers`; Concept Fan, concept: `attach volunteering to something people already need`; Fractionation, fractions: `food-safety briefing + 1,200 Facebook followers + coordinator's four hours`; Alternatives, alternative: `onboarding briefing → a recognised food-hygiene certificate that newcomers can put on a CV`; SCAMPER, operator: `Put to another use`; Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Pay volunteers in references, not money`
**Movement:** Ski instructors are mostly young people who come for a season because it gives them a qualification and experience; the property "seasonal work that builds a CV" moved to volunteering as a credential path rather than charity alone. / Challenge: Replaced gratitude with practical value as the reward. Retirees volunteer for purpose and company, but students, career-changers and the unemployed weigh time against what it gives them, and a reference or certificate costs the food bank almost nothing. / Reversal: Originally volunteers serve the food bank and get a thank-you once a year. Reversing who serves whom asks what the food bank can give its volunteers. Under-50s often need references and proof of skills, and Second Shelf can provide them for almost nothing, so giving them becomes the reason people join. / Concept Fan: Purpose → direction "recruit groups instead of individuals" → the concept looks for a need volunteers already have (a CV line, a reference) → the food-safety briefing, a constraint, becomes the hook, offered as a credential. / Fractionation: Detaching the briefing from its role as a cost the newcomer pays and attaching it to the followers as something they get turned a gate into a draw; the coordinator's hours go into one batch session a month rather than many one-to-ones. / Alternatives: The briefing has to happen anyway to keep food safe. Once it became a qualification worth having outside the food bank, the compulsory step turned into the reason under-50s sign up. / SCAMPER: The element was "the food-safety briefing", currently a barrier every newcomer has to clear. Used for a different purpose and audience, it becomes a reward that young people want for their careers. Some of the €3,000 budget covers certification costs, and volunteers are still not paid. / Six Hats: White: the brief already requires a food-safety briefing and runs real logistics weekly, which are checkable skills; Red: young job seekers feel volunteering is a sacrifice, so frame it as a credential they earn; Black: people may leave once they have the reference, so set the track at 12 Saturdays and invite graduates to become shift leads; Yellow: the greatest value is a credential with real market weight, so tie it to the supermarket partners who hire → unpaid labour became a career asset that attracts under-50s on their own terms.
**Problem it solves:** It gives under-50s a self-interested reason to join and stay a full season, directly feeding the "a third under 50" target.
**Convergence:** Random Entry, Challenge, Reversal, Concept Fan, Fractionation, Alternatives, SCAMPER, Six Hats

## 4. Bindings that release

**Description:** A formal "release" option lets any volunteer pause for up to six months, through illness, caring duties or a bad winter, without leaving the rota or redoing the briefing; the coordinator checks in once before the pause ends. Older regulars who can no longer lift move to seated roles (intake desk, phoning households, stock lists) rather than stepping away.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `ski`
**Movement:** Ski bindings are designed to release in a fall so the skier is not hurt and can clip back in; volunteers now "fall" out of the rota permanently when life gets hard, so a designed release-and-reclip keeps them from being lost.
**Problem it solves:** It cuts the eight-a-year losses at the source, mostly among the 30 over-65 regulars, so the rota holds at 40.

## 5. Skis come in pairs

**Description:** Each over-65 regular is paired with a younger "partner ski" who works the same station alongside them for a season and learns their know-how: which supermarket manager to call, how the van loads, which households need home delivery. When the older volunteer steps back, the role passes on instead of disappearing.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `ski`; Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the volunteers who are leaving choose and train their replacements.`; Challenge, assumption challenged: `when a volunteer leaves, the coordinator has to find a replacement from scratch`; Reversal, reversal applied: `the leaving volunteers recruit the new ones`; Concept Fan, concept: `let current volunteers hand on their place`; Fractionation, fractions: `grief of a lost regular + over-65 regulars' know-how + the awkward first shift`; SCAMPER, operator: `Adapt`
**Movement:** Skis only work as a matched pair moving together; the rota's knowledge sits in single older people, so pairing each one with a younger twin makes succession built in rather than a scramble after a departure. / Provocation: Extract a principle: the provocation works because the handover is controlled by the person with the most knowledge and trust. In practice this becomes personal, one-to-one recruiting by the 30 over-65s, which is warmer than any public appeal. / Challenge: Dropped the idea that departures are sudden losses for the coordinator to fill. If leaving becomes a planned handover the older volunteer owns, the person who knows the job and the community best does the recruiting and training, and the expected wave of retirements turns into a pipeline. / Reversal: Originally the food bank recruits to fill the gaps left by departing volunteers. Reversing it puts the person leaving in charge of filling their own gap. Older regulars know plenty of younger people, and a personal ask from them carries more weight than a poster, so the moment of loss becomes the moment of recruitment. / Concept Fan: Purpose → direction "grow volunteers from inside the existing community" → the concept treats each departing volunteer as a succession event rather than a loss → a paired apprenticeship of a younger person with each older regular. / Fractionation: Reordering the "grief of a lost regular" in time, so the exit comes before it happens rather than after, turned a departure into a recruiting moment; joining it to the leaver's know-how and the newcomer's awkward first shift made one person the answer to both. / SCAMPER: The idea comes from theatre understudies and craft apprenticeships, where key roles always have a trained successor in waiting. Applied to the 30 older regulars, each expected departure becomes a handover rather than a gap, and veterans become recruiters through their own personal networks.
**Problem it solves:** It turns the ageing core from a risk into the training channel for the under-50 volunteers the rota needs.
**Convergence:** Random Entry, Provocation, Challenge, Reversal, Concept Fan, Fractionation, SCAMPER

## 6. Après-shelf lunch

**Description:** Every Saturday distribution ends with a shared lunch cooked in the warehouse from that day's surplus, open to everyone who worked and one guest they bring along. The guest-of-a-volunteer rule makes the lunch the main low-pressure way people first see the place from the inside.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `ski`
**Movement:** For many skiers, après-ski is half the reason they go: the social hour after the slopes. Making the end of the shift the social payoff, with a plus-one built in, turns volunteers' friends into a steady flow of people trying it out.
**Problem it solves:** It makes Saturdays socially rewarding enough to retain people and recruits through personal networks rather than broadcast appeals.

## 7. Group lift bookings

**Description:** Local groups that already exist, such as a running club, a five-a-side team, a parents' WhatsApp group or a workplace team, "book a lift": they take over one whole station on one Saturday a month as a crew. The group handles its own internal swaps, so the coordinator manages one contact instead of eight individuals.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `ski`; Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the rota is staffed by groups, and no individual ever volunteers alone.`; Challenge, assumption challenged: `volunteers join and are rostered one person at a time`; Reversal, reversal applied: `groups volunteer, not individuals`; Concept Fan, concept: `make an organisation, not a person, the volunteer`; Fractionation, fractions: `Saturday parcel hand-out + parish congregation + coordinator's four hours`; Alternatives, alternative: `unit of commitment → a whole team adopts one Saturday a month`; SCAMPER, operator: `Substitute`; Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Treat the Saturday shift as a team sport, not an individual commitment`
**Movement:** A chairlift carries people up in seats of four who arrive together; recruiting ready-made groups instead of single people moves younger crews in whole and makes their own social bond the reason they keep coming. / Provocation: Focus on the difference: with groups as the unit, the coordinator recruits four teams instead of forty people, and the group itself supplies the social pull. Letting groups adopt dates alongside the individual rota gets that benefit for real. / Challenge: Replaced the individual with an existing group as the unit of commitment. Groups already have a shared calendar, peer pressure to turn up and an age profile far younger than the current pool, so retention comes from the group's own ties, not from the food bank. / Reversal: Originally the rota is built one person at a time, and each person has to commit alone. Flipping the unit from person to group means one yes brings in eight people. Workplaces and clubs skew younger and already have rosters and a reason to do things together, so they bring in under-50s and their organisation runs the rota. / Concept Fan: Purpose (reliable hands) → direction "recruit groups instead of individuals" → the concept moves the unit of commitment from a person to an organisation that refills its own roster → a monthly adopted Saturday per organisation. / Fractionation: Cutting the Saturday hand-out by group rather than by person, and joining it to the coordinator's four hours, showed that the scarce resource is scheduling time; handing scheduling to the groups themselves lets the parish and other groups supply whole crews at once. / Alternatives: The fixed point is enough hands on every Saturday, not enough individuals. Moving the unit from a person to a group made the team, not the coordinator, responsible for turning up, and pointed toward groups made up mostly of working-age people. / SCAMPER: The element was "the individual volunteer who signs up alone". Swapping the person for a ready-made group brings in people who already meet each week, are mostly under 50, and keep each other turning up. It also lifts the scheduling load off the coordinator's four hours. / Six Hats: White: Saturday distribution is fixed and the warehouse work is physical, and club members are mostly under 50; Red: athletes respond to team challenge and rivalry more than to a charity appeal, so frame it as a fixture with a leaderboard; Black: one club pulling out would leave a hole, so rotate four clubs across the month and keep regulars on the counter; Yellow: the greatest value is a whole squad of under-50s arriving at once, so make the club the unit of recruitment → an individual volunteer ask became a recurring team fixture that recruits younger people in blocks.
**Problem it solves:** It brings in under-50s by the group and keeps the coordinator's workload within four hours a week, growing the rota faster than single recruits can.
**Convergence:** Random Entry, Provocation, Challenge, Reversal, Concept Fan, Fractionation, Alternatives, SCAMPER, Six Hats
