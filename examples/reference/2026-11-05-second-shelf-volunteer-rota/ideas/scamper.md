# SCAMPER ideas

## 1. Adopt-a-Saturday crews

**Description:** Instead of recruiting individuals, Second Shelf asks groups such as a five-a-side team, a university society, a choir or an office team to "adopt" one Saturday a month and staff it as a crew. Each group names a captain who handles its own internal rota, so the coordinator manages about eight captains instead of dozens of separate diaries.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Substitute`
**Movement:** The element was "the individual volunteer who signs up alone". Swapping the person for a ready-made group brings in people who already meet each week, are mostly under 50, and keep each other turning up. It also lifts the scheduling load off the coordinator's four hours.
**Problem it solves:** One adopting group can add 6–10 younger people at once, which helps recruitment catch up with the drop-out rate.
**Merged into:** ideas/random-entry.md › 7. Group lift bookings

## 2. Saturday kids' corner so parents can serve

**Description:** The parish runs a supervised reading and play corner in its hall during distribution, so parents aged 30–45 can do a shift while their children are looked after nearby. The corner is staffed by older regulars who would rather do lighter work than lift crates.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Combine`
**Movement:** The element was "the Saturday morning shift", which clashes with parents' childcare. Merging it with something from outside the situation, a children's activity session, removes the main reason young parents can't come. Putting older volunteers in the corner also keeps them involved in work that suits them better.
**Problem it solves:** It brings in the under-50 age group the rota is missing, and the over-65 regulars stay on longer in roles they can sustain.
**Merged into:** ideas/alternatives.md › 3. Saturday crèche, run by the elders

## 3. Run it like parkrun

**Description:** Second Shelf copies parkrun's volunteer model: a public list of named Saturday roles (greeter, van runner, parcel packer, queue host), a simple online slot-claim, and milestone tokens at 10, 25 and 50 shifts. People are invited to "do your bit once every few weeks" rather than commit every Saturday.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Adapt`
**Movement:** The problem has a shape (a fixed weekly Saturday event run entirely by unpaid people who need regular replacing) that parkrun has already solved at scale. Bringing over its named micro-roles, its low-commitment rhythm and its visible milestones gives Second Shelf a tested way to keep volunteers.
**Problem it solves:** Lower commitment and visible progress make it easier to sign up and harder to drift away, which works on both the recruitment side and the loss side.

## 4. The four-Saturdays pledge

**Description:** Second Shelf shrinks the ask down to "four Saturdays a year, you pick which", and lets people sign up for all four dates at once. A bigger pool of light volunteers fills the rota, and some of them gradually become regulars.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Modify`
**Movement:** The element modified was "how often a volunteer is expected to serve". Making it much rarer turns a big ongoing commitment into a small, bounded one that busy working-age people can say yes to. The rota then depends on having many people rather than on each person coming often.
**Problem it solves:** It turns many people who would never become weekly regulars into reliable capacity, which helps reach 40 active volunteers.

## 5. Food-safety briefing as a free CV credential

**Description:** Second Shelf upgrades its compulsory food-safety briefing into a short recognised food-hygiene certificate and promotes it to students, job-seekers and people moving into hospitality or retail: "volunteer three Saturdays, leave with a certificate and a reference". The partner supermarkets can endorse it or treat it as a hiring plus.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Put to another use`
**Movement:** The element was "the food-safety briefing", currently a barrier every newcomer has to clear. Used for a different purpose and audience, it becomes a reward that young people want for their careers. Some of the €3,000 budget covers certification costs, and volunteers are still not paid.
**Problem it solves:** It gives under-30s a concrete reason to join, which goes straight at the age gap.
**Merged into:** ideas/random-entry.md › 3. Ski-school credentials

## 6. Turn up and serve: no sign-up step

**Description:** Second Shelf removes the application-and-scheduling stage altogether: anyone over 18 who arrives at 8:30 on a Saturday gets the short safety briefing on the spot from a trained regular and is given a role that morning. Whether someone becomes a regular is decided afterwards, once they have had a good first shift.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Eliminate`
**Movement:** The element removed was "the sign-up and onboarding funnel between interest and first shift", where many would-be volunteers drop out. Without it, getting someone to come once is all the recruitment there is, and the experience on the day does the persuading.
**Problem it solves:** It stops losing interested people before their first shift and so lifts the recruitment rate above the drop-out rate.
**Merged into:** ideas/provocation.md › 2. Turn up first, sign up later

## 7. Recipients become hosts

**Description:** Second Shelf invites adults from the 220 households it serves to take paid-forward roles such as queue host, translator or parcel packer, with no obligation, as a way to be part of the place rather than only a visitor. Their shifts sit next to their own collection time, so they don't need an extra trip.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Reverse`
**Movement:** The flip was on "the role of the household", turning a receiver into a provider. That opens a pool of hundreds of adults, many of them younger, who already come every Saturday and know how the distribution works from the queue side.
**Problem it solves:** It draws on a large, local and younger pool that recruitment never reaches, adding volunteers without any extra outreach.
**Merged into:** ideas/provocation.md › 1. Neighbour shifts for parcel households

## 8. Understudy pairing for every veteran

**Description:** Each over-65 regular is paired with a younger "understudy" who shadows their role for a few Saturdays, then alternates with them, then takes over when the veteran steps back. Veterans recruit their own understudies from family, neighbours and former colleagues.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Adapt`
**Movement:** The idea comes from theatre understudies and craft apprenticeships, where key roles always have a trained successor in waiting. Applied to the 30 older regulars, each expected departure becomes a handover rather than a gap, and veterans become recruiters through their own personal networks.
**Problem it solves:** It replaces older volunteers with younger ones before they leave, so retirements no longer drain the rota.
**Merged into:** ideas/random-entry.md › 5. Skis come in pairs
