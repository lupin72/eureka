# Reversal ideas

## 1. Pass on the apron

**Description:** Each volunteer who is cutting down or stepping away picks their own replacement, a neighbour, grandchild or colleague under 50, and works their last few Saturdays beside them. The rota tracks pairs instead of single names, so a departure comes with a successor already trained by the person leaving.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the leaving volunteers recruit the new ones`
**Movement:** Originally the food bank recruits to fill the gaps left by departing volunteers. Reversing it puts the person leaving in charge of filling their own gap. Older regulars know plenty of younger people, and a personal ask from them carries more weight than a poster, so the moment of loss becomes the moment of recruitment.
**Problem it solves:** It turns each of the eight yearly losses into a handover, so recruitment keeps up with attrition.
**Merged into:** ideas/random-entry.md › 5. Skis come in pairs

## 2. Adopt-a-Saturday teams

**Description:** Local employers, including the two partner supermarkets' own staff, a university society or a sports club, each adopt one Saturday a month and staff it as a team. The coordinator manages about 12 team captains instead of dozens of individuals, and each team does its own internal scheduling.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `groups volunteer, not individuals`
**Movement:** Originally the rota is built one person at a time, and each person has to commit alone. Flipping the unit from person to group means one yes brings in eight people. Workplaces and clubs skew younger and already have rosters and a reason to do things together, so they bring in under-50s and their organisation runs the rota.
**Problem it solves:** It fills the rota in blocks of working-age people and needs little of the coordinator's four hours.
**Merged into:** ideas/random-entry.md › 7. Group lift bookings

## 3. The briefing goes on tour

**Description:** A veteran volunteer delivers the short food-safety briefing at lunchtimes in offices, colleges and the parish hall, so whole groups qualify before they ever visit the warehouse. Each attendee leaves with a "cleared to volunteer" card and a QR link to book a first shift.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the warehouse comes to the volunteer`
**Movement:** Originally prospective volunteers have to come to Second Shelf to be briefed and start. Making the fixed place move takes the required first step to where people already are. Once the one formal hurdle is cleared at their desk, the only step left is turning up, and long-serving volunteers get a new role as trainers.
**Problem it solves:** It removes the biggest entry barrier for busy younger people, so more of them actually start volunteering.

## 4. Try a Saturday, decide later

**Description:** Anyone can come for a single guest shift with no sign-up and no rota commitment: briefing in the first 20 minutes, then working alongside a host volunteer. Only after their third guest shift are they asked whether they want a regular slot.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the experience comes before the commitment`
**Movement:** Originally people commit to a rota first and find out what it's like afterwards, and that upfront promise puts off anyone with an unpredictable schedule. Putting the experience first lets the Saturday sell itself. By the time someone is asked to commit, they already know the team and the work.
**Problem it solves:** It turns curious one-off visitors into regulars, especially younger people who won't sign up blind.
**Merged into:** ideas/provocation.md › 2. Turn up first, sign up later

## 5. Volunteering that pays forward into a CV

**Description:** Second Shelf gives volunteers something they can take away: a skills record (warehouse logistics, stock rotation, van driving, team leading), a signed reference after 10 shifts, and a LinkedIn-ready certificate. This is pitched at students, job-seekers and career-changers.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the food bank serves the volunteers`
**Movement:** Originally volunteers serve the food bank and get a thank-you once a year. Reversing who serves whom asks what the food bank can give its volunteers. Under-50s often need references and proof of skills, and Second Shelf can provide them for almost nothing, so giving them becomes the reason people join.
**Problem it solves:** It gives working-age people a concrete personal reason to join and stay, at almost no cost.
**Merged into:** ideas/random-entry.md › 3. Ski-school credentials

## 6. Shifts that fit the person

**Description:** Alongside Saturday distribution, the warehouse offers 90-minute weekday evening packing and sorting slots, Tuesday to Friday, that volunteers book whenever they're free. Saturday then needs fewer hands because parcels arrive pre-packed.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the shift moves to fit the volunteer`
**Movement:** Originally the time is fixed (Saturday morning) and people have to bend their lives around it. Making the time the thing that moves fits people with weekend jobs or young children. Distribution stays on Saturday, but packing can move into the Tuesday-to-Friday opening the warehouse already has.
**Problem it solves:** It opens volunteering to people who can never give a Saturday, and it lowers Saturday's staffing needs.
**Merged into:** ideas/provocation.md › 6. After-work warehouse evenings

## 7. Neighbours who were helped, helping

**Description:** Households that receive parcels, and are over 18, are invited to join a "neighbour shift", framed as being part of the community rather than paying something back. Many are of working age and already come every Saturday, so they are the closest pool of potential volunteers.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the households give to the food bank`
**Movement:** Originally help flows one way, from volunteers to the 220 households. Sending it the other way shows that the people already in the queue every Saturday are a large, younger group nobody asks. Some of them would welcome the chance to be on the other side of the table.
**Problem it solves:** It draws on about 220 households that already come every Saturday, many of working age.
**Merged into:** ideas/provocation.md › 1. Neighbour shifts for parcel households

## 8. The newcomer runs the Facebook page

**Description:** The newest under-30 volunteer each quarter takes over the Facebook page and films short behind-the-scenes clips of Saturdays, with the veterans as the stars. The clips show the food bank to younger people the way they'd tell a friend about it.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the new volunteer speaks for the food bank`
**Movement:** Originally the organisation speaks and potential volunteers listen. Reversing who speaks hands the channel to the newest member. Their friends and contacts are exactly the age group Second Shelf is missing, so content made by a peer reaches them through their own networks.
**Problem it solves:** It reaches under-50s through the people they already trust instead of through recruitment calls.
