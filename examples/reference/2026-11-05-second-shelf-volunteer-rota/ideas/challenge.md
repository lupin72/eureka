# Challenge ideas

## 1. Adopt-a-Saturday squads

**Description:** Second Shelf stops recruiting individuals and offers each Saturday of the month to a ready-made group, such as a five-a-side team, a company department, a university society or a running club. Each squad takes the same Saturday every month as one unit, briefs its own members through the food-safety session together, and finds its own cover from within. Twelve squads of six to eight people fill the rota with mostly under-50s, and the coordinator manages twelve contacts instead of forty people.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `volunteers join and are rostered one person at a time`
**Movement:** Replaced the individual with an existing group as the unit of commitment. Groups already have a shared calendar, peer pressure to turn up and an age profile far younger than the current pool, so retention comes from the group's own ties, not from the food bank.
**Problem it solves:** It brings in many younger volunteers at once and holds onto them through the group's own loyalty, which slows the losses that outpace recruitment.

## 2. Hand-down shifts

**Description:** Each of the 30 over-65 regulars is asked to pass on their particular role (van driver, intake desk, cold store) to a named apprentice they help recruit, working alongside them for three months before stepping back to "emeritus" status if they want to. The apprenticeship is framed as the elder's legacy, not their replacement, and the handover date is agreed together.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `when a volunteer leaves, the coordinator has to find a replacement from scratch`
**Movement:** Dropped the idea that departures are sudden losses for the coordinator to fill. If leaving becomes a planned handover the older volunteer owns, the person who knows the job and the community best does the recruiting and training, and the expected wave of retirements turns into a pipeline.
**Problem it solves:** It turns the coming departures of the over-65 majority into handovers to younger people, so a leaver no longer leaves a gap.

## 3. Saturday as a CV line

**Description:** Second Shelf turns volunteering into credentials people under 50 actually need: a certificate for the food-safety briefing, logged hours for van driving and warehouse handling, signed reference letters after three months, and recognised placements for university volunteering modules and job-seekers' activity requirements. The annual thank-you event becomes a quarterly "references and certificates" handout.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `volunteers are kept by gratitude and goodwill`
**Movement:** Replaced gratitude with practical value as the reward. Retirees volunteer for purpose and company, but students, career-changers and the unemployed weigh time against what it gives them, and a reference or certificate costs the food bank almost nothing.
**Problem it solves:** It gives younger adults a personal reason to join and stay, which goodwill alone does not give them.

## 4. Weekday packing club

**Description:** Packing and sorting move out of Saturday into two weekday evening sessions at the warehouse, already open Tuesday to Saturday, run as a social "packing club" with music and a short after-work slot from 18:30 to 20:30. Saturday keeps only distribution, which needs fewer hands, while the evening crowd of working-age people who can't give up weekends packs the parcels in advance.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `volunteering means the Saturday distribution shift`
**Movement:** Dropped the link between "being a volunteer" and "being there on Saturday". Distribution has to stay on Saturday, but preparation doesn't. Splitting the work by day opens it to people whose weekends are taken by children, sport or jobs, who are mostly under 50.
**Problem it solves:** It recruits from the large group of working-age people shut out by a Saturday-only commitment, while distribution stays on Saturday.

## 5. Self-run swap board with shift captains

**Description:** The fixed weekly rota is replaced by a shared online board of single shifts that volunteers claim, drop and swap themselves, with a rotating volunteer "shift captain" each Saturday who checks the board is covered. People can give one Saturday a month, or three in a row and then none, and nobody has to explain an absence to the coordinator.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `a volunteer commits to a regular weekly slot managed by the coordinator`
**Movement:** Dropped the regular commitment and replaced the coordinator as scheduler with the volunteers themselves. Younger people avoid open-ended weekly obligations, and a self-service board fits their irregular lives while freeing the coordinator's four spare hours.
**Problem it solves:** It lowers the commitment barrier that keeps younger people away and stops burnout-driven leaving, so the active count can grow toward 40.

## 6. From the queue to the crew

**Description:** Households who collect parcels, and people who used Second Shelf in the past, are quietly invited to join the crew on their own terms, with the same briefing, the same lanyard and no difference in status. Former recipients often know the families and the neighbourhood and tend to be younger than the current volunteers, and some say helping restores a sense of standing in the community.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `the people who help and the people who are helped are two separate groups`
**Movement:** Dropped the split between giver and receiver. The 220 households every Saturday are the biggest pool of adults with a direct tie to the food bank, already standing at the door, and the food bank had never counted them as possible volunteers.
**Problem it solves:** It draws on a large, younger and highly motivated pool that ordinary recruitment never reaches.

## 7. Supermarket crew on the donation run

**Description:** The two partner supermarkets are asked to make the Saturday donation handover a staff volunteering slot: one or two employees per store ride the van back and help sort their own store's donations for a morning, counted as paid volunteering time or CSR hours by their employer. Over a year, a rotating set of store staff gets to know the food bank, and some stay on in their own time.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `donors give food and volunteers give time, and they are different people`
**Movement:** Replaced the separation between donor and volunteer with a single relationship. The supermarkets already send food on the Saturday schedule, and their mostly younger staff can come with it, so the existing partnership also brings in people.
**Problem it solves:** It uses relationships Second Shelf already has to bring a steady flow of under-50 helpers onto Saturday shifts at no cost.
