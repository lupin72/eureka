# Provocation ideas

Provocation type: `distortion`

## 1. Neighbour shifts for parcel households

**Description:** Households who collect parcels are invited to take a "neighbour shift" on the Saturday line or in the warehouse. It is strictly opt-in and has no effect on their parcel. Many of the 220 households include working-age adults with a direct stake in the service, so the food bank can recruit from people who are already in the building every week.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the households receiving parcels run the Saturday distribution.`; Challenge, assumption challenged: `the people who help and the people who are helped are two separate groups`; Reversal, reversal applied: `the households give to the food bank`; Concept Fan, concept: `make the people who receive the hands that give`; Fractionation, fractions: `220 households in the queue + the waiting hour in the queue + food-safety briefing`; SCAMPER, operator: `Reverse`; Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `The 220 households in the queue are already a Saturday crowd`
**Movement:** Positive aspects: if recipients ran distribution, the workforce would be the people who know the service best and turn up every Saturday. The food bank can keep that benefit by opening a voluntary route from receiving to helping, without handing anyone the whole operation. / Challenge: Dropped the split between giver and receiver. The 220 households every Saturday are the biggest pool of adults with a direct tie to the food bank, already standing at the door, and the food bank had never counted them as possible volunteers. / Reversal: Originally help flows one way, from volunteers to the 220 households. Sending it the other way shows that the people already in the queue every Saturday are a large, younger group nobody asks. Some of them would welcome the chance to be on the other side of the table. / Concept Fan: Purpose → direction "grow volunteers from inside the existing community" → the concept flips the 220 households from clients to a recruitment pool already present each Saturday → an opt-in crew invitation at the distribution line. / Fractionation: Detaching the households from their role as recipients and joining them to the idle waiting hour turned dead time into the briefing slot, and the people already on site into the recruitment pool. / SCAMPER: The flip was on "the role of the household", turning a receiver into a provider. That opens a pool of hundreds of adults, many of them younger, who already come every Saturday and know how the distribution works from the queue side. / Six Hats: White: about 220 households come every Saturday and the food bank already has their attention at the right time; Red: recipients may feel ashamed or obliged, so make the offer anonymous in look and fully optional; Black: any hint of conditionality would damage trust, so keep the ask separate from the parcel and say so explicitly; Yellow: the value is a ready pool of people who know the operation from the other side, so put welcome and language roles at the centre → a service population became a volunteer pool that is already on site on Saturdays.
**Problem it solves:** It opens a younger recruitment pool that comes in every week and that the current rota, mostly over 65, never reaches.
**Convergence:** Provocation, Challenge, Reversal, Concept Fan, Fractionation, SCAMPER, Six Hats

## 2. Turn up first, sign up later

**Description:** Anyone can walk in on a Saturday at 8:30, do the short food-safety briefing on the spot, and work a supervised first shift with no form to fill in and no commitment. Only after the shift are they asked whether they want a regular slot, while the experience is still fresh.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO volunteers sign up after their shift, not before.`; Reversal, reversal applied: `the experience comes before the commitment`; Concept Fan, concept: `shrink the commitment unit`; SCAMPER, operator: `Eliminate`
**Movement:** Moment to moment: walking through a Saturday where sign-up comes last shows that people drop off between showing interest and doing a first shift. Putting the shift first removes that step, and the question about commitment comes when a yes is most likely. / Reversal: Originally people commit to a rota first and find out what it's like afterwards, and that upfront promise puts off anyone with an unpredictable schedule. Putting the experience first lets the Saturday sell itself. By the time someone is asked to commit, they already know the team and the work. / Concept Fan: Purpose → direction "reshape the work to fit working-age lives" → the concept cuts the unit of commitment from a standing weekly shift to a single hour with no planning → a drop-in slot that works as a funnel into the rota. / SCAMPER: The element removed was "the sign-up and onboarding funnel between interest and first shift", where many would-be volunteers drop out. Without it, getting someone to come once is all the recruitment there is, and the experience on the day does the persuading.
**Problem it solves:** It turns casual interest into first shifts and then regular volunteers, which helps close the gap between eight lost and two recruited.
**Convergence:** Provocation, Reversal, Concept Fan, SCAMPER

## 3. Pass the apron

**Description:** Each long-serving volunteer is asked to bring in one successor, ideally under 50, from their family, neighbours or former colleagues. They train that person and work alongside them for a few months before passing on their regular slot. The handover becomes a small ritual recorded on the rota board.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the volunteers who are leaving choose and train their replacements.`
**Movement:** Extract a principle: the provocation works because the handover is controlled by the person with the most knowledge and trust. In practice this becomes personal, one-to-one recruiting by the 30 over-65s, which is warmer than any public appeal.
**Problem it solves:** Each departing volunteer is replaced before they leave, so the rota stops shrinking as older volunteers retire.

## 4. Adopt-a-Saturday teams

**Description:** A workplace, sports club, student society or choir adopts one Saturday a month and fills a block of slots as a team, arranging its own cover. Second Shelf briefs the group once and gives it a named slot on the rota.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the rota is staffed by groups, and no individual ever volunteers alone.`
**Movement:** Focus on the difference: with groups as the unit, the coordinator recruits four teams instead of forty people, and the group itself supplies the social pull. Letting groups adopt dates alongside the individual rota gets that benefit for real.
**Problem it solves:** It brings in younger volunteers in batches for very little coordinator time, which fits the four spare hours a week.

## 5. Supermarket staff on the sorting shift

**Description:** Second Shelf asks its two partner supermarkets to count the time their staff spend sorting donations at the warehouse as employer-supported volunteering or team-building hours. Staff come in on weekday afternoons, when the warehouse is open but short of hands, and some go on to volunteer on Saturdays in their own time.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the supermarkets' own staff do the food bank's work.`; Challenge, assumption challenged: `donors give food and volunteers give time, and they are different people`; Fractionation, fractions: `supermarket staff + van supermarket run + food-safety briefing`; Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Let the food bring its own volunteers with it`
**Movement:** Special circumstances: the provocation is useful wherever a donor already employs people who have a reason to care. The brief has exactly that in its good relations with two supermarkets, so an existing donation link becomes a source of working-age volunteers. / Challenge: Replaced the separation between donor and volunteer with a single relationship. The supermarkets already send food on the Saturday schedule, and their mostly younger staff can come with it, so the existing partnership also brings in people. / Fractionation: Detaching the van run from Second Shelf's own volunteer block and attaching it to the supermarket staff who already stand at the other end of it showed a ready pool of working-age people for whom the food-safety gate is already open. / Six Hats: White: Second Shelf already has good relations with two supermarkets and its Saturday schedule is built around their donations; Red: staff feel more ownership when they see where their store's food goes, so make it "follow your food"; Black: Saturday is a busy retail day, so ask for only two staff per store and let the stores pick the rota; Yellow: the value is a renewable source of food-trained, mostly working-age people, so make the donation partnership include people as well as food → a food supply relationship became a volunteer supply relationship.
**Problem it solves:** It brings under-50 helpers onto the rota without Second Shelf paying anyone, because the employer chooses to release its own staff.
**Convergence:** Provocation, Challenge, Fractionation, Six Hats

## 6. After-work warehouse evenings

**Description:** Some of the weekday warehouse work (sorting, packing, checking dates) moves to one or two evening slots from 18:00 to 20:00, so that people with day jobs can help. Saturday distribution stays as it is; the evenings prepare for it.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the food bank works only when its volunteers are free, not when it is open.`; Challenge, assumption challenged: `volunteering means the Saturday distribution shift`; Concept Fan, concept: `move the work to the hours younger people have free`; Reversal, reversal applied: `the shift moves to fit the volunteer`
**Movement:** Extract a principle: in the provocation, the volunteers' free time sets the schedule, not the opening hours. Saturday distribution has to stay fixed, but the Tuesday-to-Friday warehouse work can move to hours when working-age people are free. / Challenge: Dropped the link between "being a volunteer" and "being there on Saturday". Distribution has to stay on Saturday, but preparation doesn't. Splitting the work by day opens it to people whose weekends are taken by children, sport or jobs, who are mostly under 50. / Concept Fan: Purpose → direction "reshape the work to fit working-age lives" → the concept splits the work by when it must happen: distribution is tied to Saturday, packing is not → packing shifts to weekday evenings when working adults are free. / Reversal: Originally the time is fixed (Saturday morning) and people have to bend their lives around it. Making the time the thing that moves fits people with weekend jobs or young children. Distribution stays on Saturday, but packing can move into the Tuesday-to-Friday opening the warehouse already has.
**Problem it solves:** It removes the timing barrier that keeps under-50s, who mostly work on weekdays, off a rota built around daytime hours.
**Convergence:** Provocation, Challenge, Concept Fan, Reversal

## 7. Ninety-minute micro-slots

**Description:** The Saturday rota is cut into 90-minute slots that people can take one at a time, through a simple shared sign-up sheet linked from the Facebook page. Regulars can stack several slots, while newcomers commit to one at a time.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO volunteers sign up after their shift, not before.`; Alternatives, alternative: `unit of commitment → 90-minute micro-shifts booked like a gym class`; Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Sell the rota like fitness classes, by the slot`
**Movement:** Focus on the difference: if sign-up came after the shift, the first commitment would cost almost nothing. Cutting the commitment into small pieces keeps that cost low while still giving the coordinator a plan. / Alternatives: Holding "enough hands per Saturday" as the fixed point let the commitment shrink from being a regular to booking a slot. Borrowing from gym booking made a short, flexible pledge feel normal to people with busy weekends. / Six Hats: White: the coordinator has four hours a week and the shift structure is fixed to Saturday, so the format must run itself; Red: younger people balk at open-ended commitments but book short classes easily; Black: short slots could leave gaps at handover, so overlap them by 15 minutes and let the coordinator see the grid days ahead; Yellow: the greatest value is lowering the first-step cost, so build the "try one slot" path as the front door → a fixed rota became a self-service grid that converts casual tryers into regulars.
**Problem it solves:** It lowers the commitment that puts busy younger people off a full-morning rota, so more people become active volunteers.
**Convergence:** Provocation, Alternatives, Six Hats
