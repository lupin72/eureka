# Random Entry ideas

Random word: `honeycomb`

## 1. Shared-wall weeks

**Description:** The planner lays out the week so each dinner shares at least one perishable ingredient with the next, like hexagonal cells that share walls with their neighbours. Half a bag of spinach from Monday becomes Tuesday's frittata, and the shopping list shows how much it shrank because of the shared walls. Users feel the plan saving them food and money in week two, the point where they usually stop.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `honeycomb`
**Movement:** Cells share walls, so nothing is built twice. Dinners could share ingredients the same way, which turns the plan from a list of recipes into a chain that wastes less. The saving is a reason to keep planning.
**Problem it solves:** It gives new users a concrete, felt payoff in their first weeks, so planning a third week is worth the effort.

## 2. Winter stores

**Description:** In the first weeks Pantry suggests doubling two dinners and freezing the extra portions, and it logs them as "capped cells" in a freezer stock. When week three or four comes, the app fills two or three nights from those stores, so the user only has to plan the rest.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `honeycomb`
**Movement:** Bees fill and cap cells with honey for lean months. Early cooking could fill stores for the weeks when motivation drops, so the third week costs less effort than the first.
**Problem it solves:** It cuts the planning and cooking load right at the two-to-three-week drop-off point.

## 3. Nectar tray

**Description:** A capture-anywhere tray (share sheet, screenshot, a typed craving, "chicken thighs on offer") collects bits of dinner ideas during the week. On planning day the tray has "ripened": Pantry matches each item to library recipes and drafts most of the week from them, and the user only confirms.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `honeycomb`
**Movement:** Nectar is collected bit by bit and ripens in the cell before it becomes honey. Planning could also build up over the week instead of starting from a blank page every Sunday.
**Problem it solves:** It removes the blank-page effort that makes users give up on planning after a week or two.

## 4. Brood night

**Description:** One dinner a week is marked as the kids' night. Pantry picks a simple recipe from the library and shows it as a kid-scaled card with big steps and jobs by age. The children start asking "what's our night this week?", which pulls the parent back into planning.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `honeycomb`
**Movement:** Brood cells are where the young are raised inside the comb. Giving the household's young their own cell in the week builds a family ritual that depends on the plan.
**Problem it solves:** It ties the weekly plan to a family habit, so stopping would mean breaking a ritual, not just closing an app.

## 5. Harvest frame

**Description:** At the end of each month the app shows a "harvest frame": dinners cooked, euros not spent on takeaway, and ingredients used up rather than thrown away, estimated from the plans and lists. Users can export it as a one-page image for the fridge.
**Technique:** Random Entry, random word: `honeycomb`
**Movement:** The beekeeper pulls out a frame to see and collect what the hive produced. Users never see what their planning produced, so a frame makes that output visible.
**Problem it solves:** It shows new users the value they have built up, which gives them a reason to keep planning in month two.
