# Provocation ideas

Provocation type: `distortion`

## 1. What we actually ate

**Description:** At the end of each week Pantry asks one 20-second question in the app: which of the planned dinners did you actually cook, which did you swap, which did you skip? Next week's plan is pre-filled from what really happened, so the plan bends to the household's real behaviour instead of the household failing the plan.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the household plans the week after they have eaten it.`
**Movement:** Focus on the difference: a plan written after eating always matches reality, so nobody feels they "failed" it. The early drop-off after week one or two looks like the moment plan and reality diverge; recording reality and planning from it keeps that gap from ever opening.
**Problem it solves:** It removes the guilt-and-abandon moment after the first planned weeks that drives the 40% early churn.
**Merged into:** ideas/scamper.md › 7. Cooked-it check-in first

## 2. The house rotation

**Description:** After two or three weeks Pantry turns the dinners a household cooked and liked into a personal rotation, and the default next week is "your rotation, with one new dish". Planning becomes accepting or tweaking a familiar week rather than choosing from 3,000 recipes.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the household plans the week after they have eaten it.`
**Movement:** Extract a principle: if past meals write the plan, the past is the planner. Most households eat about ten dinners on repeat; making that repertoire the starting point turns the weekly effort into a two-tap confirmation.
**Problem it solves:** It cuts the weekly planning effort that new users stop paying once the novelty wears off.
**Merged into:** ideas/scamper.md › 4. Plan once, rotate a month

## 3. Start from the fridge

**Description:** Pantry carries forward the ingredients bought last week but not used (the shopping list minus the dinners cooked) and opens week two with "you still have half a bag of spinach and a pack of chorizo: here are three dinners that use them". The week is built around what is already in the house before anything new is listed.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the shopping is done first and the dinners are chosen from whatever was bought.`
**Movement:** Moment to moment: imagine buying first and planning second; the planner's first step becomes "what do I have?", which is the exact question a household faces at the start of week two with leftovers from week one. Pantry already knows the list, so it can answer it.
**Problem it solves:** It gives users a concrete, money-saving reason to open the app at the point where they usually stop.
**Merged into:** ideas/scamper.md › 2. Leftover-led second week

## 4. Receipt-in planning

**Description:** Users can photograph a supermarket receipt or paste an online grocery order, and Pantry proposes the week's dinners from what was bought, then adds only the few missing items. People who shop on impulse or out of habit get value without changing how they shop.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the shopping is done first and the dinners are chosen from whatever was bought.`
**Movement:** Special circumstances: buying first is already what many households do on a busy week, which is exactly when a plan-first app gets abandoned. An off-the-shelf OCR service fits the budget and lets the app meet those weeks instead of losing them.
**Problem it solves:** It keeps users who "fell off the plan" one week from leaving altogether, because Pantry still works when they shopped first.
**Merged into:** ideas/challenge.md › 1. Basket-first week

## 5. Everyone owns a night

**Description:** The account holder can hand a night to another household member through a shared link that needs no account: a partner takes Tuesday, a teenager picks Friday from a short shortlist. Pantry shows the week with each person's name on their night and adds their picks to the shopping list.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the people who eat the dinners plan them, and the person who shops only buys.`; Random Entry, random word: `honeycomb`
**Movement:** Positive aspects: if eaters plan, the planner is no longer alone with the chore and others start to expect the app's week to exist. Keeping that good means splitting nights, so the habit lives in the household rather than in one tired person. / Random Entry: No single bee builds the comb; the whole hive adds cells. Weekly planning often falls on one person, who burns out, so spreading the cells across the household shares the load and the ownership.
**Problem it solves:** It spreads the planning load and creates household expectation, so one person's lapse in motivation no longer ends the app's use.
**Convergence:** Provocation, Random Entry

## 6. A rolling three-day strip

**Description:** Instead of a blank week to fill every seven days, Pantry shows a continuous strip of the next three dinners that always rolls forward: each day one slot drops off and a new one is suggested at the end. Shopping lists are built for whatever window the user chooses to shop for.
**Technique:** Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the week has no start and no end.`
**Movement:** Focus on the difference: without week boundaries there is no "start a new week" moment, and the brief shows drop-off at exactly those boundaries after the first or second week. A rolling strip removes the cliff and the empty grid that has to be filled from scratch.
**Problem it solves:** It eliminates the weekly restart point where new users most often stop planning.
**Merged into:** ideas/challenge.md › 2. Three-night horizon
