# Challenge ideas

## 1. Basket-first week

**Description:** The user does their usual shop first, then photographs the receipt or ticks off what they bought from a quick grid, and Pantry builds the week's dinners from what is already in the bag. Planning stops being a homework task done before shopping and becomes a two-minute sort done after it.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `dinners are planned before the shopping is done`; Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the shopping is done first and the dinners are chosen from whatever was bought.`
**Problem it solves:** Users who drop off because weekly planning feels like a chore keep using Pantry because it fits around the shop they already do.
**Convergence:** Challenge, Provocation

## 2. Three-night horizon

**Description:** Instead of a seven-dinner plan, Pantry plans only the next three dinners and re-plans every few days from what was actually cooked. Each mini-plan takes seconds and is rarely wrong, so it never goes stale mid-week.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `dinners are planned for a whole week at once`; Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the week has no start and no end.`
**Problem it solves:** It removes the heavy weekly planning session that new users abandon after their first or second planned week.
**Convergence:** Challenge, Provocation

## 3. Family veto link

**Description:** The planner shares a plain web link of the draft week with the rest of the household, who can tap thumbs-up, veto, or "my turn to cook" on each dinner without installing anything. The planner opens Pantry to see the verdicts and finalise the week.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `one person in the household plans and decides dinners alone`
**Problem it solves:** Planning becomes a household ritual with others waiting on it, so the planner keeps returning past the third week.

## 4. Dinner calendar feed

**Description:** Pantry publishes the week's dinners as a subscribable calendar feed, so each evening's meal and its prep note appear in the household's existing calendars next to work and school events. Tapping an event opens that dinner in Pantry to swap or adjust it.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `users get value only when they open the app`
**Problem it solves:** It keeps the plan visible between sessions, so users don't forget Pantry in the weeks when attention fades.

## 5. Skip-proof week

**Description:** The plan includes flex nights, and any dinner that wasn't cooked rolls forward automatically with its ingredients, instead of the week showing as "missed". The weekly summary celebrates what was cooked rather than flagging what wasn't.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `the household cooks dinners exactly as planned`
**Problem it solves:** It prevents the sense of failure that makes new users abandon planning after a week that didn't go to plan.

## 6. Cook-swap pairs

**Description:** Pantry pairs two nearby households who opt in; each cooks a double batch of one planned dinner per week and swaps a portion, with the swap dish built into both plans. The pair's shared schedule lives in Pantry.
**Technique:** Challenge, assumption challenged: `each household plans and cooks on its own`
**Problem it solves:** A standing arrangement with another household gives new users an outside reason to keep planning with Pantry for months.
