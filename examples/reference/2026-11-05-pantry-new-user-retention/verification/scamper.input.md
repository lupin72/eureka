# SCAMPER ideas

## 1. Household planning rota

**Description:** After their first planned week, the new user is asked to name who plans next week: a partner, flatmate or teenager, who joins through a simple invite link. Each week the planning passes to the next person on the household rota, and Pantry shows whose turn it is and what they picked.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Substitute`
**Problem it solves:** Users drop out after one or two weeks because one person carries all the planning, and spreading that turn across the household keeps the account active past week three.

## 2. Leftover-led second week

**Description:** When a user starts planning week two, Pantry first lists the items from last week's shopping list that never went into a cooked dinner (half a cabbage, the rest of the rice) and proposes dinners from the library that use them up. The new week starts from what is already in the kitchen, not from a blank screen.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Combine`; Challenge, assumption challenged: `each planned week is independent of the one before`; Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the shopping is done first and the dinners are chosen from whatever was bought.`
**Problem it solves:** It gives users a concrete, waste-saving reason to come back for the second and third week, exactly when 40% of them stop.
**Convergence:** SCAMPER, Challenge, Provocation

## 3. Four-week cooking seasons

**Description:** New users sign up for a "season": a four-week themed arc such as "One-pan weeknights" or "Ten new vegetables", built from the existing 3,000 recipes, with a visible episode count and a finale dinner in week four. When a season ends, the next one is offered as a fresh start.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Adapt`
**Problem it solves:** It gives the fragile weeks two and three a narrative pull, carrying new users through to the second month.

## 4. Plan once, rotate a month

**Description:** Instead of planning every week from scratch, the new user plans a single session that Pantry turns into a four-week rotating menu, repeating favourites with small variations. Each week they only swap the dinners they are tired of, which takes a minute instead of a full planning session.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Modify`; Challenge, assumption challenged: `the app's value is discovering new recipes from its library`; Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the household plans the week after they have eaten it.`; Random Entry, random word: `honeycomb`
**Problem it solves:** It cuts the weekly planning cost that drives drop-off in the first three weeks, so staying with Pantry for two months becomes the default.
**Convergence:** SCAMPER, Challenge, Provocation, Random Entry

## 5. The household's own cookbook

**Description:** Every dinner a household actually cooks and rates is added to a growing personal cookbook inside Pantry, with their own notes ("double the garlic", "kids loved it"). By week eight the cookbook becomes the household's go-to source for planning, and leaving Pantry means leaving it behind.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Put to another use`
**Problem it solves:** It makes each extra week of planning more valuable than the last, giving new users a reason to stay through the second month.

## 6. Library-free first month

**Description:** For the first four weeks, new users do not browse the 3,000-recipe library; they plan only from the dozen dinners they already cook, entered at sign-up in a few words each. The library opens gradually afterwards, one suggested recipe per week next to their regulars.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Eliminate`
**Problem it solves:** Early weeks become easy wins built on dinners users already make, so they keep planning past week three instead of quitting after an overreaching first plan.

## 7. Cooked-it check-in first

**Description:** The app opens on yesterday, not next week: a single tap to say what the household actually ate last night, whether planned or not. Pantry builds the next week's plan from these check-ins, so planning becomes a by-product of a five-second daily habit.
**Technique:** SCAMPER, operator: `Reverse`; Provocation, PO (distortion): `PO the household plans the week after they have eaten it.`
**Problem it solves:** It replaces the heavy weekly planning task that new users drop with a light daily touch that keeps them in the app through two months.
**Convergence:** SCAMPER, Provocation
