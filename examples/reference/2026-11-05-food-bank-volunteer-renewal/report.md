# Report: Second Shelf volunteer renewal

## Techniques

| Technique | Skill | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reversal | `lateral-reversal` | The problem has clear actors and flows (volunteers serve households, the food bank recruits individuals, leavers are replaced after they go), so flipping who does what gives a view from the other side. |
| Fractionation | `lateral-fractionation` | Second Shelf has many existing pieces (ageing regulars, warehouse days, van, supermarkets, parish, recipients) treated as one fixed Saturday block. |
| Six Hats | `lateral-six-hats` | Several stakeholders and feelings are involved (veterans who fear being pushed out, recipients, newcomers), so raw ideas need shaping against facts and risks before verification. |

**Skipped:** none

## Shortlist

### 1. Adopt-a-Saturday crews

**Card:** ideas/reversal.md › 1. Adopt-a-Saturday crews
**Source:** Reversal, Six Hats (converged)
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and far from the baseline: the rota recruits ready-made groups instead of individuals, so one yes fills a whole Saturday; reached by Reversal and Six Hats.
**Movement:** The original direction is that the food bank recruits individuals and the coordinator fits each one into the rota. Reversed, the rota is handed out in blocks and the group does the scheduling. That moves the coordination work off the coordinator's four hours and into groups that already meet and already skew younger, so one "yes" brings in six to ten people. / Six Hats: White: distribution is fixed on Saturdays for about 220 households, so a monthly crew slot fits the one non-negotiable day; Red: under-50s join things with friends, not alone, so the unit became the group they already belong to; Black: one absent person breaks an individual rota, so the captain covers swaps inside the crew and the coordinator's time is protected; Yellow: the greatest value is that one "yes" brings in six people, so the crew is the unit of recruitment → individual volunteering became a team sport.
**First step:** List five local groups (a five-a-side team, a choir, a running club, a workplace team, a parish youth group) and ask two of them this week to adopt one Saturday next month.

### 2. Grandparent corner

**Card:** ideas/fractionation.md › 2. Grandparent corner
**Source:** Fractionation
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and turns the ageing problem into the solution: older regulars move to a children's corner, which frees parents under 50 to work the line.
**Movement:** Joining the veterans' Saturday habit with the parents' Saturday problem made one solve the other. The ageing regulars stop being a loss and become the thing that makes the shift possible for younger families.
**First step:** Ask the three regulars who find lifting hardest whether they would run a Saturday children's corner, and check the parish's safeguarding policy covers it.

### 3. Fixed-term tours instead of open-ended membership

**Card:** ideas/reversal.md › 7. Fixed-term tours instead of open-ended membership
**Source:** Reversal
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and changes the commitment itself from open-ended to a six-month cohort with a send-off, which the baseline never touches.
**Movement:** The original direction is to join first and leave whenever, which looks like a lifelong commitment from outside and puts off busy under-50s. Putting the ending first makes the ask finite and safe. Cohorts give the coordinator one onboarding per batch, and the renewal point at six months turns quiet drop-outs into a decision people make actively.
**First step:** Draft a one-page description of a six-month "tour" starting in January and post it on the Facebook page with a sign-up form capped at ten.

### 4. Bookable Micro-Shifts

**Card:** ideas/six-hats.md › 3. Bookable Micro-Shifts
**Source:** Six Hats
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ and lets working-age people help in 90-minute slots around the fixed regulars, replacing the coordinator's phone calls with a booking page.
**Movement:** White: Saturday is the only distribution day and under-50s often have children's activities or work on part of it; Red: a whole Saturday feels like too big a promise, while a 90-minute slot feels easy, so the slot became the unit; Black: fragmented slots could leave gaps, so a live shortage view nudges bookings toward empty stations and veterans anchor the long shifts; Yellow: the value is lowering the entry cost to near zero, so the path from first slot to regular slot became the centre → a fixed rota became a flexible, self-filling timetable.
**First step:** Set up a free booking page with next Saturday split into four 90-minute slots per station and share it with current volunteers first.

### 5. Gift a Saturday

**Card:** ideas/six-hats.md › 2. Gift a Saturday
**Source:** Six Hats
**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Why it ranks here:** ✅ but the closest to the baseline of the walkable ideas: it still invites individuals, through people they already know.
**Movement:** White: the Facebook page has 1,200 followers who already care but don't volunteer, and the supermarkets see heavy seasonal footfall; Red: being asked to volunteer feels like a demand, receiving an invitation from a friend feels like being chosen, so the shift became a gift; Black: cards could go unused, so each card carries a fixed date and a named buddy who follows up; Yellow: the value lies in turning passive followers into recruiters of their own circles, so the giver works the first shift too → recruitment became a social gift between friends.
**First step:** Print 50 "Saturday at Second Shelf" cards at low cost and give five to each of the ten most active regulars to hand on.

### 6. Elders train the apprentices

**Card:** ideas/reversal.md › 3. Elders train the apprentices
**Source:** Reversal, Fractionation, Six Hats (converged)
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Why it ranks here:** 🔧, and the strongest convergence (Reversal, Fractionation, Six Hats): the leavers recruit and train their own successors, so know-how is handed on rather than lost; it needs each elder to bring a successor from their own circle.
**Movement:** The original direction is that the organisation replaces volunteers after they leave. Reversed, the person leaving names and trains their successor before they go. That turns the 30 older regulars from a risk into the people doing the recruiting: they bring in their children, neighbours and former colleagues, and the know-how is handed on instead of lost. / Fractionation: Reordering put the leaving moment before the recruiting moment instead of after it. Then joining the guilt veterans feel about quitting to their know-how turned departure into a recruiting event that each leaver owns. / Six Hats: White: 30 of 45 regulars are over 65, so the people most likely to leave are also the best-connected recruiters; Red: "replace yourself" feels like being pushed out, so it became "pass on your apron" with the veteran staying as mentor; Black: the coordinator's four hours can't run 30 inductions, so veterans deliver the briefing and the coordinator only checks it; Yellow: the real value is continuity of know-how, so the shift lineage on the wall puts that at the centre → a departure became a planned, personal handover.
**First step:** Ask the five regulars most likely to step back next year to name one younger person from their own life they could bring to a Saturday.

### 7. Households on the other side of the table

**Card:** ideas/reversal.md › 2. Households on the other side of the table
**Source:** Reversal, Fractionation (converged)
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Why it ranks here:** 🔧 and a different source of volunteers altogether (the households served), reached by Reversal and Fractionation; it needs a group briefing so the coordinator's hours hold.
**Movement:** The original direction is volunteers serving recipients. Reversing it turns 220 households into the largest pool of people who already turn up every Saturday, many of them of working age. The step that makes it workable is a role that sits next to collecting a parcel, not in place of it, so taking part costs no extra trip. / Fractionation: Detaching people from the "served" side of the Saturday block and attaching them to the "serving" side removed the line between the two. Being familiar with the place takes away the newcomer's main fear.
**First step:** Put a no-pressure "help with the queue welcome?" note in next Saturday's parcels, unlinked to the parcels, with the date of a group briefing.

### 8. Supermarket staff on the delivery side

**Card:** ideas/reversal.md › 6. Supermarket staff on the delivery side
**Source:** Reversal, Fractionation (converged)
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Why it ranks here:** 🔧 and recruits through the donors' own staff, reached by Reversal and Fractionation; it has to move to weekday sorting because Saturdays are peak trading days.
**Movement:** The original direction is that food flows one way, from supermarket to food bank, and the donor's part ends at the loading bay. Reversed, the donor follows the flow all the way to the table. That turns an existing relationship into a source of people, and retail staff may already hold food-hygiene training that the briefing can build on. / Fractionation: Detaching the supermarket tie from the donation block, where it only supplies food, and attaching it to the idle weekday warehouse made it supply people as well, at no cost to Second Shelf.
**First step:** Ask the community lead of each partner supermarket whether staff could take one weekday sorting slot a month as paid community time.

## Appendix

### The hygiene certificate as a front door

**Card:** ideas/fractionation.md › 1. The hygiene certificate as a front door
**Source:** Fractionation, Six Hats (converged)
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 3. Second Shelf cannot issue a recognised food-hygiene certificate by itself, because hospitality food-hygiene training has to come from an accredited provider. Paying an accredited trainer every month would also strain the €3,000 budget and the coordinator's four hours. Fix: Partner with a local catering school or accredited training provider that co-delivers and co-signs one session a quarter at a reduced or donated fee. Between those sessions, give each graduate a signed volunteer-hours reference letter alongside the in-house briefing.

### Friday Night Pack

**Card:** ideas/fractionation.md › 3. Friday Night Pack
**Source:** Fractionation
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 3. Food for 20 to 30 people once a month costs roughly €2,000 to €3,000 a year. That uses up most or all of the €3,000 budget, and the cost rises as the evening succeeds. Fix: Ask the two partner supermarkets to donate the pizza and drinks as a sponsored evening, or make it bring-a-dish, so the budget pays only for occasional extras.

### Pop-up shifts where young people already are

**Card:** ideas/reversal.md › 4. Pop-up shifts where young people already are
**Source:** Reversal
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 5: staffing several weekday pop-ups would take more than the coordinator's four spare hours a week. The pop-ups also end with nothing that turns a passer-by into a regular, so the rota does not become stable. Fix: Run one pop-up a fortnight, led by a regular volunteer who already drives the van, and end every session with a sign-up for a specific Saturday or weekday slot.

### Thanks first, ask later

**Card:** ideas/reversal.md › 5. Thanks first, ask later
**Source:** Reversal
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 2: guests who help on the spot have not had the food-safety briefing, and taking regulars off distribution to serve breakfast stretches an already thin Saturday team. Fix: Hold the breakfast just before distribution starts, fit the 10–15 minute food-safety briefing into the behind-the-scenes tour, and let only adults who have been briefed stay to help.

### Warehouse Desk Days

**Card:** ideas/six-hats.md › 1. Warehouse Desk Days
**Source:** Six Hats
**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Note:** Step 3: visitors sort and label food from their first visit, but the idea only asks for the food-safety briefing before a Saturday shift. That breaks the constraint that every volunteer passes the briefing, and it also leaves the over-18 check undone. Fix: Make the short food-safety briefing, with an age check, the price of entry to desk days: run it at the start of each visitor's first visit, so no one handles food unbriefed and every desk-day regular is already cleared for Saturdays.
