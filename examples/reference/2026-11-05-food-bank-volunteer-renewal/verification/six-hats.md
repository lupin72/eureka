## ideas/six-hats.md

### 1. Warehouse Desk Days

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. A steady flow of under-50 remote workers who already know the warehouse and the team, converted onto Saturday shifts, adds younger regulars to the rota and works toward 40 active volunteers with a third under 50.
**Backward chain:**

1. Desk-day regulars have moved onto the Saturday rota, pushing it toward 40 active volunteers with at least a third under 50.
2. A group of remote workers uses the warehouse on Tuesday to Friday and gives an hour of sorting, labelling or stock-checking on each visit.
3. Remote workers have heard about desk days through the Facebook page and posters in the partner supermarkets, and have come for a first visit, where they handle food before anyone has briefed them.
4. The warehouse has trestle tables, chairs, Wi-Fi and a coffee machine, plus a short list of weekday sorting tasks for desk-day visitors.
5. About €600 of the €3,000 has gone on a year of broadband and the kit, and the coordinator has used some spare hours to set up the space and write the task list.
6. Today: the warehouse is open Tuesday to Saturday, and Second Shelf has the €3,000 budget, the coordinator's four spare hours a week and the Facebook page.

**Broken link:** Step 3: visitors sort and label food from their first visit, but the idea only asks for the food-safety briefing before a Saturday shift. That breaks the constraint that every volunteer passes the briefing, and it also leaves the over-18 check undone.
**Possible fix:** Make the short food-safety briefing, with an age check, the price of entry to desk days: run it at the start of each visitor's first visit, so no one handles food unbriefed and every desk-day regular is already cleared for Saturdays.

### 2. Gift a Saturday

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. Each gift brings someone new, often a younger friend or relative, to a guided first Saturday with a named buddy. A first shift with a buddy is the start most likely to turn into a regular slot, which feeds the rota's size and its under-50 share.
**Backward chain:**

1. Gift receivers have done a guided first Saturday with their buddy, and enough of them have stayed on to grow the rota and lower its average age.
2. Receivers have booked a briefing slot and a first Saturday with a named buddy. The booking checks that they are over 18, and the briefing is done before the shift.
3. Existing volunteers and supporters have picked up cards and given them to friends and relatives for Christmas and birthdays.
4. Cards are stocked at the two partner supermarkets ahead of Christmas and promoted on the Facebook page.
5. A few hundred cards have been printed for around €100–200. The coordinator, within the four spare hours a week, has set dates for group briefings and a list of willing buddies, and the supermarkets have agreed to put the cards out.
6. Today: Second Shelf has good relations with two supermarkets, a Facebook page with 1,200 followers, current regulars who can act as buddies, and the €3,000 budget.

### 3. Bookable Micro-Shifts

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. Short slots booked by phone lower the barrier for busy under-50s, and the route from six slots to a fixed slot turns occasional helpers into regulars. Together these grow the Saturday rota toward 40 and raise its under-50 share, while older regulars keep their long shifts.
**Backward chain:**

1. The Saturday rota runs as 90-minute slots around the regulars' fixed shifts. Newcomers who have booked six slots have taken fixed slots, and the rota is heading toward 40 active volunteers with a third under 50.
2. Briefed newcomers book one or two slots at a time from their phones and can see which stations are short.
3. A booking page is live with each station broken into 90-minute slots and the older regulars' fixed shifts already filled in. The link is pinned on the Facebook page and given out at each food-safety briefing.
4. The coordinator has split the Saturday stations into 90-minute slots and set them up in a free booking tool, using some of the four spare hours a week. Free tools that show open slots per station already exist.
5. Today: Second Shelf has the coordinator, an existing Saturday rota to map, the Facebook page and the briefing process.
