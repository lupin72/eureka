# Reversal ideas

## 1. Adopt-a-Saturday crews

**Description:** Instead of filling the rota one person at a time, Second Shelf offers each Saturday of the month to a ready-made group: a five-a-side team, a choir, a workplace department, a running club. The group commits to one Saturday a month, sorts out who comes among themselves, and the coordinator gives one food-safety briefing to the whole crew at once.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the rota recruits groups, and groups fill the rota themselves`; Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Volunteer as a team, the way you'd play in a league`
**Problem it solves:** It raises recruitment in batches of under-50s while costing the coordinator almost no extra hours.
**Convergence:** Reversal, Six Hats

## 2. Households on the other side of the table

**Description:** Invite the households who collect parcels to take a shift whenever they want, starting with an easy role such as welcoming people in the queue or running the children's corner. Over time they become a regular team with a name of their own and a say in how Saturdays are run.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the recipients serve the food bank`; Fractionation, fractions: `220 parcel recipients + Saturday queue + awkwardness of first shift`
**Problem it solves:** It recruits from a large, younger, already-present group the food bank currently treats only as receivers.
**Convergence:** Reversal, Fractionation

## 3. Elders train the apprentices

**Description:** Each over-65 regular takes on one or two "apprentices" under 50 for a season, teaching them the van route, the supermarket pickups or the warehouse system. The apprenticeship has a clear end: the apprentice takes over that job, and the older volunteer moves to a lighter role or a mentoring one.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the volunteers who are leaving recruit their own replacements`; Fractionation, fractions: `retiring hands + guilt of quitting + veteran's know-how`; Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Volunteers don't retire, they bequeath their shift to a successor`
**Problem it solves:** It replaces the leavers before they leave and keeps their knowledge in the food bank.
**Convergence:** Reversal, Fractionation, Six Hats

## 4. Pop-up shifts where young people already are

**Description:** On Tuesday to Friday, take part of the warehouse work out of the warehouse: sort and pack donations in the van parked at a university, a gym car park or a big employer's site at lunch or after work. Passers-by can join a 45-minute shift on the spot, with the food-safety briefing given as a short in-person talk before they start.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the work goes to the volunteers instead of volunteers coming to the work`
**Problem it solves:** It reaches younger people who would never seek out the warehouse, using the van and the weekday opening hours.

## 5. Thanks first, ask later

**Description:** Run a small "Saturday breakfast" each month that is open to anyone: coffee and pastries from the partner supermarkets, served by the current volunteers, and a look behind the scenes of the distribution. Guests aren't asked to sign up; they are just invited to stay and help for an hour if they feel like it.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the food bank gives to the volunteer before the volunteer gives`
**Problem it solves:** It lowers the threshold for new volunteers by putting belonging before commitment.

## 6. Supermarket staff on the delivery side

**Description:** Propose to the two partner supermarkets that their staff take part in the Saturday distribution of the food they donated, as a team-building or community-hours scheme run by the store. The supermarket gets a visible local story; Second Shelf gets a rotating crew of working-age people who already handle food safely.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the donors follow their food to the households`; Fractionation, fractions: `two supermarket ties + Tuesday–Friday warehouse`
**Problem it solves:** It turns existing partners into a recurring source of under-50 volunteers with no recruitment spend.
**Convergence:** Reversal, Fractionation

## 7. Fixed-term tours instead of open-ended membership

**Description:** Offer volunteering as a six-month "tour" with a start date, a goal and a send-off, not an open-ended commitment. People join in cohorts of eight to ten, train together, and at the end choose to sign up for another tour, move into a lead role, or leave with a reference letter and a certificate.
**Technique:** Reversal, reversal applied: `the exit is planned before the entry`
**Problem it solves:** It makes volunteering feel doable for younger, busier people and turns retention into a regular renewal.
