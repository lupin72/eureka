## ideas/reversal.md

### 1. Adopt-a-Saturday crews

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Four or five adult crews of 6–10 people, each owning one Saturday a month, put 30–40 people on a stable rota. Most of the groups named (sports teams, running clubs, workplace departments) are mostly under 50, so the end state meets both the headcount and the age targets.
**Backward chain:**

1. Each Saturday of the month is staffed by an adopted crew alongside the remaining regulars, and the rota holds at least 40 active volunteers.
2. Four or five groups have each agreed to one Saturday a month, named a crew lead who fills the shift, and confirmed that every member who comes is over 18.
3. Each crew has had one group food-safety briefing from the coordinator (about an hour per crew, spread over several weeks, within the four spare hours).
4. The coordinator has pitched the offer to 10–15 local clubs, choirs and employers by email, the Facebook page and the parish, and the regulars have passed it to the groups they belong to. Both can start today at no cost.

### 2. Households on the other side of the table

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** A named team of recipient-volunteers who show up regularly adds people to the rota from a pool of 220 households that includes working-age adults, so it can help with both numbers and age. That only holds if the team becomes a regular rota rather than occasional drop-ins.
**Backward chain:**

1. A named team of recipient-volunteers works regular Saturday shifts and has a say in how distribution runs.
2. Recipients have done several Saturday shifts and asked for regular slots.
3. Recipients have started taking a shift "whenever they want" in easy roles (queue welcome, children's corner).
4. Recipients have been invited during the Saturday queue, and the invitation makes clear their parcel never depends on helping. This can be done today.

**Broken link:** Step 3: when recipients join "whenever they want" on the spot, newcomers start without the food-safety briefing, and briefing each one separately would quickly use up the coordinator's four spare hours a week.
**Possible fix:** Before anyone takes a first shift, require a fixed monthly group briefing (for example 30 minutes after distribution on the first Saturday) open only to adults, and list the briefed recipients as rota members.

### 3. Elders train the apprentices

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** If each over-65 regular hands their job to one or two under-50 apprentices and stays on in a lighter role, the rota grows past 40 and gets younger, and knowledge is kept. That matches the Desired state.
**Backward chain:**

1. Apprentices under 50 have taken over the van route, the supermarket pickups and the warehouse jobs, and the older regulars have moved into lighter or mentoring roles.
2. Apprentices have completed a season shadowing their mentor and have passed the food-safety briefing. Those taking the van are covered by the van's insurance.
3. Each of the 30 over-65 regulars has been matched with one or two apprentices for the season.
4. Some 30–60 people under 50 have come forward wanting to be apprentices.
5. The coordinator has run a drive to find them.

**Broken link:** Step 5: finding 30–60 under-50 apprentices through the coordinator brings back the original recruitment problem, and the coordinator's four spare hours a week cannot cover it.
**Possible fix:** Ask each older regular to bring their own successor from their own circle (a grandchild, neighbour, former colleague or fellow parishioner) and to keep the matching to themselves, while the coordinator only gives the group briefing and checks that the van insurance covers the new drivers.

### 4. Pop-up shifts where young people already are

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** The pop-ups reach younger people, but a 45-minute one-off helper on a weekday is not a member of a stable rota. The idea only meets the Desired state if the pop-ups lead people into regular shifts.
**Backward chain:**

1. Regular weekday pop-ups at a university, a gym and an employer bring a steady flow of under-50s, and some of them join the rota.
2. The van runs pop-up sorting sessions on fixed weekdays, each run by someone who gives the short briefing and supervises the work.
3. Host sites have agreed to the van parking there, and there is a hygienic way to sort food in or beside the van.
4. Someone has the hours to staff two or three pop-ups a week (about 2 hours each, plus travel).
5. The coordinator runs the pop-ups.

**Broken link:** Step 5: staffing several weekday pop-ups would take more than the coordinator's four spare hours a week. The pop-ups also end with nothing that turns a passer-by into a regular, so the rota does not become stable.
**Possible fix:** Run one pop-up a fortnight, led by a regular volunteer who already drives the van, and end every session with a sign-up for a specific Saturday or weekday slot.

### 5. Thanks first, ask later

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** A monthly open breakfast is a low-threshold way in, and guests who stay on become rota members. It does not target under-50s, so it helps the headcount more clearly than the age mix.
**Backward chain:**

1. Guests from the monthly breakfast have become regular volunteers.
2. Breakfast guests have stayed to help for an hour on distribution Saturdays.
3. A monthly Saturday breakfast runs at the warehouse, with pastries and coffee donated by the partner supermarkets and served by current volunteers.
4. The supermarkets have agreed to donate breakfast items, and some regulars have agreed to host. This can be asked today.

**Broken link:** Step 2: guests who help on the spot have not had the food-safety briefing, and taking regulars off distribution to serve breakfast stretches an already thin Saturday team.
**Possible fix:** Hold the breakfast just before distribution starts, fit the 10–15 minute food-safety briefing into the behind-the-scenes tour, and let only adults who have been briefed stay to help.

### 6. Supermarket staff on the delivery side

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** A store-backed crew of working-age staff on a fixed rotation adds stable, under-50 volunteers without spending on recruitment, which fits the Desired state.
**Backward chain:**

1. Crews from the two supermarkets regularly staff Saturday distribution.
2. The stores have set up a community-hours scheme that releases staff for Saturday shifts.
3. Store management has agreed to the proposal in exchange for a local story.
4. Second Shelf has pitched it to its contacts at the two stores, which it can do today.

**Broken link:** Step 2: Saturday is a peak trading day, so the stores are unlikely to release staff then, and distribution must stay on Saturdays.
**Possible fix:** Ask for staff who are off that Saturday to volunteer under the scheme, or have store crews take the Tuesday-to-Friday pickups and warehouse sorting so that regulars are free for Saturday.

### 7. Fixed-term tours instead of open-ended membership

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Cohorts of 8–10 people on six-month tours, with a reference letter and certificate that appeal to younger, busier people, add volunteers in groups and turn retention into a planned renewal. That supports a stable rota of 40 with a younger profile.
**Backward chain:**

1. Successive six-month cohorts keep the rota at 40 or more, with renewals and new cohorts replacing those who leave.
2. The first cohort has finished its tour, and most have renewed or moved into lead roles.
3. A cohort of 8–10 has started together on a fixed date and done one shared food-safety briefing.
4. The tour has been announced on the Facebook page (1,200 followers), in the parish newsletter and in the partner supermarkets, with a start date and a certificate template costing next to nothing. This can be done today within the budget and the coordinator's hours.
