## ideas/fractionation.md

### 1. The hygiene certificate as a front door

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. Students and job-seekers are mostly under 50, and a supervised Saturday shift built into the course puts them on the line, so this targets both the size of the rota and its age mix.
**Backward chain:**

1. Every month a group of students and job-seekers finishes the course, works its supervised Saturday shift, and some of them stay on the rota.
2. The course is advertised where under-50s look for it: the 1,200-follower Facebook page, the local job centre, colleges and the hospitality schools.
3. Employers accept the certificate as a valid food-hygiene credential for hospitality jobs.
4. The coordinator has turned the existing food-safety briefing into a short monthly course and fits it into four spare hours a week.
5. The coordinator already runs the briefing for every new volunteer, so this step can start today.

**Broken link:** Step 3. Second Shelf cannot issue a recognised food-hygiene certificate by itself, because hospitality food-hygiene training has to come from an accredited provider. Paying an accredited trainer every month would also strain the €3,000 budget and the coordinator's four hours.
**Possible fix:** Partner with a local catering school or accredited training provider that co-delivers and co-signs one session a quarter at a reduced or donated fee. Between those sessions, give each graduate a signed volunteer-hours reference letter alongside the in-house briefing.

### 2. Grandparent corner

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. It works on both sides of the Problem. Older regulars who would otherwise leave stay active, and under-50 parents who were kept away by Saturday childcare can join the rota.
**Backward chain:**

1. Every Saturday older regulars run a play and homework corner while parents under 50 work the distribution line a few metres away.
2. Parents with young children have been recruited, using the corner as the selling point, through the Facebook page, the parish and local schools.
3. The corner is set up in a part of the warehouse kept away from the line and the van, with donated toys and books, a sign-in and sign-out sheet, a rule that two adults are always present, and the volunteer insurance confirmed or extended to cover minors on site. The cost is well within €3,000.
4. Several older regulars who struggle with crates have agreed to switch roles.
5. Today the coordinator can raise the idea with the regulars on a Saturday and phone the insurer. Both fit in the four spare hours.

### 3. Friday Night Pack

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. It gives working people a way in that fits their week and then moves them onto the Saturday rota. That raises numbers and lowers the average age.
**Backward chain:**

1. Once a month, a Friday evening session from 7 to 9 pm packs Saturday's parcels with a crowd of working-age volunteers, and some of them move onto the Saturday rota.
2. Every newcomer takes the food-safety briefing in the first part of the session before handling food.
3. Each session offers music and pizza for 20 to 30 people, paid from the budget.
4. The session is promoted on the Facebook page as a social evening rather than a commitment.
5. The warehouse opens on Friday evenings with a key-holder (the coordinator or a veteran) in charge. Only stock already delivered by Friday is pre-packed, so the supermarkets' Saturday donation schedule is left as it is.
6. The warehouse already operates on Fridays, and the coordinator can schedule a first evening today.

**Broken link:** Step 3. Food for 20 to 30 people once a month costs roughly €2,000 to €3,000 a year. That uses up most or all of the €3,000 budget, and the cost rises as the evening succeeds.
**Possible fix:** Ask the two partner supermarkets to donate the pizza and drinks as a sponsored evening, or make it bring-a-dish, so the budget pays only for occasional extras.
