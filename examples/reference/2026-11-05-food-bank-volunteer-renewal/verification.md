# Verification

## ideas/reversal.md

### 1. Adopt-a-Saturday crews

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Four or five adult crews of 6–10 people, each owning one Saturday a month, put 30–40 people on a stable rota. Most of the groups named (sports teams, running clubs, workplace departments) are mostly under 50, so the end state meets both the headcount and the age targets.
**Backward chain:**

1. Each Saturday of the month is staffed by an adopted crew alongside the remaining regulars, and the rota holds at least 40 active volunteers.
2. Four or five groups have each agreed to one Saturday a month, named a crew lead who fills the shift, and confirmed that every member who comes is over 18.
3. Each crew has had one group food-safety briefing from the coordinator (about an hour per crew, spread over several weeks, within the four spare hours).
4. The coordinator has pitched the offer to 10–15 local clubs, choirs and employers by email, the Facebook page and the parish, and the regulars have passed it to the groups they belong to. Both can start today at no cost.

### 2. Households on the other side of the table

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** A named team of recipient-volunteers who show up regularly adds people to the rota from a pool of 220 households that includes working-age adults, so it can help with both numbers and age. That only holds if the team becomes a regular rota rather than occasional drop-ins.
**Backward chain:**

1. A named team of recipient-volunteers works regular Saturday shifts and has a say in how distribution runs.
2. Recipients have done several Saturday shifts and asked for regular slots.
3. Recipients have started taking a shift "whenever they want" in easy roles (queue welcome, children's corner).
4. Recipients have been invited during the Saturday queue, and the invitation makes clear their parcel never depends on helping. This can be done today.

**Broken link:** Step 3: when recipients join "whenever they want" on the spot, newcomers start without the food-safety briefing, and briefing each one separately would quickly use up the coordinator's four spare hours a week.
**Possible fix:** Before anyone takes a first shift, require a fixed monthly group briefing (for example 30 minutes after distribution on the first Saturday) open only to adults, and list the briefed recipients as rota members.

### 3. Elders train the apprentices

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** If each over-65 regular hands their job to one or two under-50 apprentices and stays on in a lighter role, the rota grows past 40 and gets younger, and knowledge is kept. That matches the Desired state.
**Backward chain:**

1. Apprentices under 50 have taken over the van route, the supermarket pickups and the warehouse jobs, and the older regulars have moved into lighter or mentoring roles.
2. Apprentices have completed a season shadowing their mentor and have passed the food-safety briefing. Those taking the van are covered by the van's insurance.
3. Each of the 30 over-65 regulars has been matched with one or two apprentices for the season.
4. Some 30–60 people under 50 have come forward wanting to be apprentices.
5. The coordinator has run a drive to find them.

**Broken link:** Step 5: finding 30–60 under-50 apprentices through the coordinator brings back the original recruitment problem, and the coordinator's four spare hours a week cannot cover it.
**Possible fix:** Ask each older regular to bring their own successor from their own circle (a grandchild, neighbour, former colleague or fellow parishioner) and to keep the matching to themselves, while the coordinator only gives the group briefing and checks that the van insurance covers the new drivers.

### 4. Pop-up shifts where young people already are

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** The pop-ups reach younger people, but a 45-minute one-off helper on a weekday is not a member of a stable rota. The idea only meets the Desired state if the pop-ups lead people into regular shifts.
**Backward chain:**

1. Regular weekday pop-ups at a university, a gym and an employer bring a steady flow of under-50s, and some of them join the rota.
2. The van runs pop-up sorting sessions on fixed weekdays, each run by someone who gives the short briefing and supervises the work.
3. Host sites have agreed to the van parking there, and there is a hygienic way to sort food in or beside the van.
4. Someone has the hours to staff two or three pop-ups a week (about 2 hours each, plus travel).
5. The coordinator runs the pop-ups.

**Broken link:** Step 5: staffing several weekday pop-ups would take more than the coordinator's four spare hours a week. The pop-ups also end with nothing that turns a passer-by into a regular, so the rota does not become stable.
**Possible fix:** Run one pop-up a fortnight, led by a regular volunteer who already drives the van, and end every session with a sign-up for a specific Saturday or weekday slot.

### 5. Thanks first, ask later

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** A monthly open breakfast is a low-threshold way in, and guests who stay on become rota members. It does not target under-50s, so it helps the headcount more clearly than the age mix.
**Backward chain:**

1. Guests from the monthly breakfast have become regular volunteers.
2. Breakfast guests have stayed to help for an hour on distribution Saturdays.
3. A monthly Saturday breakfast runs at the warehouse, with pastries and coffee donated by the partner supermarkets and served by current volunteers.
4. The supermarkets have agreed to donate breakfast items, and some regulars have agreed to host. This can be asked today.

**Broken link:** Step 2: guests who help on the spot have not had the food-safety briefing, and taking regulars off distribution to serve breakfast stretches an already thin Saturday team.
**Possible fix:** Hold the breakfast just before distribution starts, fit the 10–15 minute food-safety briefing into the behind-the-scenes tour, and let only adults who have been briefed stay to help.

### 6. Supermarket staff on the delivery side

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** A store-backed crew of working-age staff on a fixed rotation adds stable, under-50 volunteers without spending on recruitment, which fits the Desired state.
**Backward chain:**

1. Crews from the two supermarkets regularly staff Saturday distribution.
2. The stores have set up a community-hours scheme that releases staff for Saturday shifts.
3. Store management has agreed to the proposal in exchange for a local story.
4. Second Shelf has pitched it to its contacts at the two stores, which it can do today.

**Broken link:** Step 2: Saturday is a peak trading day, so the stores are unlikely to release staff then, and distribution must stay on Saturdays.
**Possible fix:** Ask for staff who are off that Saturday to volunteer under the scheme, or have store crews take the Tuesday-to-Friday pickups and warehouse sorting so that regulars are free for Saturday.

### 7. Fixed-term tours instead of open-ended membership

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Cohorts of 8–10 people on six-month tours, with a reference letter and certificate that appeal to younger, busier people, add volunteers in groups and turn retention into a planned renewal. That supports a stable rota of 40 with a younger profile.
**Backward chain:**

1. Successive six-month cohorts keep the rota at 40 or more, with renewals and new cohorts replacing those who leave.
2. The first cohort has finished its tour, and most have renewed or moved into lead roles.
3. A cohort of 8–10 has started together on a fixed date and done one shared food-safety briefing.
4. The tour has been announced on the Facebook page (1,200 followers), in the parish newsletter and in the partner supermarkets, with a start date and a certificate template costing next to nothing. This can be done today within the budget and the coordinator's hours.

## ideas/fractionation.md

### 3. The hygiene certificate as a front door

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. Students and job-seekers are mostly under 50, and a supervised Saturday shift built into the course puts them on the line, so this targets both the size of the rota and its age mix.
**Backward chain:**

1. Every month a group of students and job-seekers finishes the course, works its supervised Saturday shift, and some of them stay on the rota.
2. The course is advertised where under-50s look for it: the 1,200-follower Facebook page, the local job centre, colleges and the hospitality schools.
3. Employers accept the certificate as a valid food-hygiene credential for hospitality jobs.
4. The coordinator has turned the existing food-safety briefing into a short monthly course and fits it into four spare hours a week.
5. The coordinator already runs the briefing for every new volunteer, so this step can start today.

**Broken link:** Step 3. Second Shelf cannot issue a recognised food-hygiene certificate by itself, because hospitality food-hygiene training has to come from an accredited provider. Paying an accredited trainer every month would also strain the €3,000 budget and the coordinator's four hours.
**Possible fix:** Partner with a local catering school or accredited training provider that co-delivers and co-signs one session a quarter at a reduced or donated fee. Between those sessions, give each graduate a signed volunteer-hours reference letter alongside the in-house briefing.

### 4. Grandparent corner

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. It works on both sides of the Problem. Older regulars who would otherwise leave stay active, and under-50 parents who were kept away by Saturday childcare can join the rota.
**Backward chain:**

1. Every Saturday older regulars run a play and homework corner while parents under 50 work the distribution line a few metres away.
2. Parents with young children have been recruited, using the corner as the selling point, through the Facebook page, the parish and local schools.
3. The corner is set up in a part of the warehouse kept away from the line and the van, with donated toys and books, a sign-in and sign-out sheet, a rule that two adults are always present, and the volunteer insurance confirmed or extended to cover minors on site. The cost is well within €3,000.
4. Several older regulars who struggle with crates have agreed to switch roles.
5. Today the coordinator can raise the idea with the regulars on a Saturday and phone the insurer. Both fit in the four spare hours.

### 5. Friday Night Pack

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. It gives working people a way in that fits their week and then moves them onto the Saturday rota. That raises numbers and lowers the average age.
**Backward chain:**

1. Once a month, a Friday evening session from 7 to 9 pm packs Saturday's parcels with a crowd of working-age volunteers, and some of them move onto the Saturday rota.
2. Every newcomer takes the food-safety briefing in the first part of the session before handling food.
3. Each session offers music and pizza for 20 to 30 people, paid from the budget.
4. The session is promoted on the Facebook page as a social evening rather than a commitment.
5. The warehouse opens on Friday evenings with a key-holder (the coordinator or a veteran) in charge. Only stock already delivered by Friday is pre-packed, so the supermarkets' Saturday donation schedule is left as it is.
6. The warehouse already operates on Fridays, and the coordinator can schedule a first evening today.

**Broken link:** Step 3. Food for 20 to 30 people once a month costs roughly €2,000 to €3,000 a year. That uses up most or all of the €3,000 budget, and the cost rises as the evening succeeds.
**Possible fix:** Ask the two partner supermarkets to donate the pizza and drinks as a sponsored evening, or make it bring-a-dish, so the budget pays only for occasional extras.

## ideas/six-hats.md

### 3. Warehouse Desk Days

**Verdict:** 🔧 Adherent, link to fix
**Adherence:** Yes. A steady flow of under-50 remote workers who already know the warehouse and the team, converted onto Saturday shifts, adds younger regulars to the rota and works toward 40 active volunteers with a third under 50.
**Backward chain:**

1. Desk-day regulars have moved onto the Saturday rota, pushing it toward 40 active volunteers with at least a third under 50.
2. A group of remote workers uses the warehouse on Tuesday to Friday and gives an hour of sorting, labelling or stock-checking on each visit.
3. Remote workers have heard about desk days through the Facebook page and posters in the partner supermarkets, and have come for a first visit, where they handle food before anyone has briefed them.
4. The warehouse has trestle tables, chairs, Wi-Fi and a coffee machine, plus a short list of weekday sorting tasks for desk-day visitors.
5. About €600 of the €3,000 has gone on a year of broadband and the kit, and the coordinator has used some spare hours to set up the space and write the task list.
6. Today: the warehouse is open Tuesday to Saturday, and Second Shelf has the €3,000 budget, the coordinator's four spare hours a week and the Facebook page.

**Broken link:** Step 3: visitors sort and label food from their first visit, but the idea only asks for the food-safety briefing before a Saturday shift. That breaks the constraint that every volunteer passes the briefing, and it also leaves the over-18 check undone.
**Possible fix:** Make the short food-safety briefing, with an age check, the price of entry to desk days: run it at the start of each visitor's first visit, so no one handles food unbriefed and every desk-day regular is already cleared for Saturdays.

### 5. Gift a Saturday

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. Each gift brings someone new, often a younger friend or relative, to a guided first Saturday with a named buddy. A first shift with a buddy is the start most likely to turn into a regular slot, which feeds the rota's size and its under-50 share.
**Backward chain:**

1. Gift receivers have done a guided first Saturday with their buddy, and enough of them have stayed on to grow the rota and lower its average age.
2. Receivers have booked a briefing slot and a first Saturday with a named buddy. The booking checks that they are over 18, and the briefing is done before the shift.
3. Existing volunteers and supporters have picked up cards and given them to friends and relatives for Christmas and birthdays.
4. Cards are stocked at the two partner supermarkets ahead of Christmas and promoted on the Facebook page.
5. A few hundred cards have been printed for around €100–200. The coordinator, within the four spare hours a week, has set dates for group briefings and a list of willing buddies, and the supermarkets have agreed to put the cards out.
6. Today: Second Shelf has good relations with two supermarkets, a Facebook page with 1,200 followers, current regulars who can act as buddies, and the €3,000 budget.

### 6. Bookable Micro-Shifts

**Verdict:** ✅ Adherent and walkable
**Adherence:** Yes. Short slots booked by phone lower the barrier for busy under-50s, and the route from six slots to a fixed slot turns occasional helpers into regulars. Together these grow the Saturday rota toward 40 and raise its under-50 share, while older regulars keep their long shifts.
**Backward chain:**

1. The Saturday rota runs as 90-minute slots around the regulars' fixed shifts. Newcomers who have booked six slots have taken fixed slots, and the rota is heading toward 40 active volunteers with a third under 50.
2. Briefed newcomers book one or two slots at a time from their phones and can see which stations are short.
3. A booking page is live with each station broken into 90-minute slots and the older regulars' fixed shifts already filled in. The link is pinned on the Facebook page and given out at each food-safety briefing.
4. The coordinator has split the Saturday stations into 90-minute slots and set them up in a free booking tool, using some of the four spare hours a week. Free tools that show open slots per station already exist.
5. Today: Second Shelf has the coordinator, an existing Saturday rota to map, the Facebook page and the briefing process.
