## 1. Shift inheritance

**Description:** A regular who plans to step back does not just leave: they "hand down" their Saturday slot to a successor they bring in and train shoulder to shoulder over four to six shifts. The handover is marked publicly at the warehouse, with the slot's name and history passed on alongside the apron.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `retiring hands + guilt of quitting + veteran's know-how`
**Movement:** Reordering put the leaving moment before the recruiting moment instead of after it. Then joining the guilt veterans feel about quitting to their know-how turned departure into a recruiting event that each leaver owns.
**Problem it solves:** It stops the eight-out, two-in leak at the point where it happens, so every departure brings in a newcomer.

## 2. Supermarket staff sorting slot

**Description:** The two partner supermarkets offer their staff, many under 50, one paid-by-the-employer sorting slot a month at the warehouse on Tuesday to Friday, as part of their community programme. Staff come in already knowing the stock, and those who enjoy it are invited onto the Saturday rota.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `two supermarket ties + Tuesday–Friday warehouse`
**Movement:** Detaching the supermarket tie from the donation block, where it only supplies food, and attaching it to the idle weekday warehouse made it supply people as well, at no cost to Second Shelf.
**Problem it solves:** It opens a steady supply of working-age volunteers without paying them, since their employer covers the time.
**Merged into:** ideas/reversal.md › 6. Supermarket staff on the delivery side

## 3. The hygiene certificate as a front door

**Description:** Second Shelf turns its compulsory food-safety briefing into a free, recognised short certificate offered monthly to anyone over 18, aimed at students and job-seekers who need a food-hygiene line on a CV for hospitality work. The course finishes with one supervised shift on the Saturday line as the practical part.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `food-safety briefing + 1,200 Facebook followers + awkwardness of first shift`; Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `A volunteer shift is a line on your CV, not a favour`
**Movement:** Detaching the briefing from onboarding, where it is a hurdle, and attaching it to the public turned it into something young people want. The first shift becomes the end of the course, which removes the awkwardness of turning up cold. / Six Hats: White: volunteers already must pass a food-safety briefing, which is a real, checkable skill, and Second Shelf has good relations with two supermarkets that hire; Red: younger people feel volunteering as a cost to their limited time, so it was reframed as an investment in their career; Black: a pure CV-builder could bring people who leave after a month, so the reference unlocks only at three months and the lead title at a full season; Yellow: the biggest value is the supermarket link, so the employer recognition became the core of the offer → a shift became a stepping stone to a job.
**Problem it solves:** It draws in under-50s who come for the certificate and stay for the rota.
**Convergence:** Fractionation, Six Hats

## 4. Grandparent corner

**Description:** Older regulars who can no longer lift crates move to running a play and homework corner on Saturdays for the children of volunteers. Parents with young children, who usually can't volunteer on Saturdays, can now work the line while their kids are looked after a few metres away.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `Saturday habit + veteran's know-how + Saturday queue`
**Movement:** Joining the veterans' Saturday habit with the parents' Saturday problem made one solve the other. The ageing regulars stop being a loss and become the thing that makes the shift possible for younger families.
**Problem it solves:** It keeps older volunteers active while removing the childcare barrier that keeps under-50 parents away.

## 5. Friday Night Pack

**Description:** One Friday evening a month, the warehouse hosts a social packing session from 7 to 9 pm with music and pizza paid from the €3,000 budget, where volunteers pre-pack Saturday's parcels. It is built for people who work all week and want company more than a commitment, and it feeds them into the Saturday rota.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `Tuesday–Friday warehouse + loneliness of an empty evening`
**Movement:** Cutting by emotion turned up the loneliness of a weeknight evening, and joining it to the warehouse's open weekday hours turned volunteering into a night out rather than a morning duty.
**Problem it solves:** It gives working-age people a way into Second Shelf that fits their week and their wish to meet people.

## 6. From the queue to the crew

**Description:** Households who receive parcels are invited, without pressure and with no link to their parcels, to join the Saturday crew for a shift. Recipients already know the routine and the regulars, so they skip the awkward first day, and the crew starts to reflect the community it serves.
**Technique:** Fractionation, fractions: `220 parcel recipients + Saturday queue + awkwardness of first shift`
**Movement:** Detaching people from the "served" side of the Saturday block and attaching them to the "serving" side removed the line between the two. Being familiar with the place takes away the newcomer's main fear.
**Problem it solves:** It recruits from 220 households already present every Saturday, many of them under 50.
