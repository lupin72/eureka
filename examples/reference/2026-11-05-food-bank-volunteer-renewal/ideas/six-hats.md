# Six Hats ideas

## 1. Hand Over Your Apron

**Description:** Each over-65 regular is asked to pass on their shift, not to leave it: they pick one younger person from their own life (a grandchild, a neighbour, a former colleague's child) and work alongside them for six Saturdays, after which the newcomer takes the slot and the veteran becomes their on-call mentor. The veteran also gives the food-safety briefing to their successor during the first shift, so the coordinator only signs off the handover. Each completed handover is marked by a name plate on the warehouse wall listing the "lineage" of who held that shift before.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Volunteers don't retire, they bequeath their shift to a successor`
**Movement:** White: 30 of 45 regulars are over 65, so the people most likely to leave are also the best-connected recruiters; Red: "replace yourself" feels like being pushed out, so it became "pass on your apron" with the veteran staying as mentor; Black: the coordinator's four hours can't run 30 inductions, so veterans deliver the briefing and the coordinator only checks it; Yellow: the real value is continuity of know-how, so the shift lineage on the wall puts that at the centre → a departure became a planned, personal handover.
**Problem it solves:** It turns each expected loss of an older volunteer into a recruitment of a younger one, closing the gap between leavers and joiners.

## 2. Adopt-a-Saturday Crews

**Description:** Ready-made groups (a five-a-side team, a choir, a running club, a workplace department, a group of friends) adopt one Saturday a month as a crew of six to eight and staff a whole station, such as packing or loading, together. Each crew names a captain who handles its own swaps, so the rota is filled by twelve crews rather than forty individuals. Crews keep a running tally of parcels packed on a board in the warehouse, and the season ends with a crew-versus-crew cook-off using surplus stock.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Volunteer as a team, the way you'd play in a league`
**Movement:** White: distribution is fixed on Saturdays for about 220 households, so a monthly crew slot fits the one non-negotiable day; Red: under-50s join things with friends, not alone, so the unit became the group they already belong to; Black: one absent person breaks an individual rota, so the captain covers swaps inside the crew and the coordinator's time is protected; Yellow: the greatest value is that one "yes" brings in six people, so the crew is the unit of recruitment → individual volunteering became a team sport.
**Problem it solves:** It brings in younger volunteers in blocks and makes the Saturday rota stable by having crews own whole dates.

## 3. Warehouse Desk Days

**Description:** On Tuesday to Friday the warehouse opens a few trestle tables with Wi-Fi as a free "desk day" space for remote workers and freelancers, who work their own jobs there and give an hour of sorting, labelling or stock-checking per visit. Desk-day regulars who pass the briefing are then invited onto a Saturday shift. A small part of the €3,000 covers the Wi-Fi, a coffee machine and a few chairs.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `The warehouse is a co-working space that happens to sort food`
**Movement:** White: the warehouse already opens Tuesday to Saturday and most working-age people under 50 are free on weekdays only if they work remotely; Red: "come and volunteer" asks for a sacrifice, while "come and work somewhere free with company" offers a perk, so the pitch leads with the desk; Black: unsupervised newcomers among food stock raise safety issues, so desk-day helpers do only non-food tasks until briefed, and the coordinator sets the space up once rather than hosting it; Yellow: the value is a weekday pipeline that feeds Saturdays, so desk days became the on-ramp to the rota → an idle weekday warehouse became a recruiting ground.
**Problem it solves:** It reaches under-50s who are lonely working from home and converts them into Saturday volunteers through a low-commitment weekday habit.

## 4. The Second Shelf Reference

**Description:** Second Shelf offers a formal, signed reference and a logged record of hours, food-safety training and responsibilities to anyone who volunteers for three months, aimed at students, people returning to work and job seekers. The two partner supermarkets agree to recognise the record and the food-safety briefing as a plus when hiring for shop-floor roles. Volunteers who lead a station for a season get a "shift lead" title they can put on a CV.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `A volunteer shift is a line on your CV, not a favour`
**Movement:** White: volunteers already must pass a food-safety briefing, which is a real, checkable skill, and Second Shelf has good relations with two supermarkets that hire; Red: younger people feel volunteering as a cost to their limited time, so it was reframed as an investment in their career; Black: a pure CV-builder could bring people who leave after a month, so the reference unlocks only at three months and the lead title at a full season; Yellow: the biggest value is the supermarket link, so the employer recognition became the core of the offer → a shift became a stepping stone to a job.
**Problem it solves:** It gives under-50s a personal reason to join and to stay for months, which raises both recruitment and retention.

## 5. Gift a Saturday

**Description:** People can give a "Saturday at Second Shelf" as a present: a printed card, made at low cost, that invites a friend or relative to a guided first shift alongside the giver. The card includes the food-safety briefing slot and a named buddy for the day, so the receiver arrives expected rather than as a stranger. Cards are sold for nothing and handed out through the supermarkets at Christmas and on birthdays shared via the Facebook page.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Volunteering is something you give to someone else`
**Movement:** White: the Facebook page has 1,200 followers who already care but don't volunteer, and the supermarkets see heavy seasonal footfall; Red: being asked to volunteer feels like a demand, receiving an invitation from a friend feels like being chosen, so the shift became a gift; Black: cards could go unused, so each card carries a fixed date and a named buddy who follows up; Yellow: the value lies in turning passive followers into recruiters of their own circles, so the giver works the first shift too → recruitment became a social gift between friends.
**Problem it solves:** It converts the food bank's existing supporters into a channel that brings new, often younger, people to a first Saturday.

## 6. Bookable Micro-Shifts

**Description:** The Saturday rota is cut into 90-minute slots that anyone briefed can book from their phone like a gym class, with a live view of which stations are short. Older regulars keep fixed long shifts while newcomers fill around them with one or two slots at a time, and a simple free booking tool replaces the coordinator's phone calls. Anyone who books six slots is invited to take a regular fixed slot.
**Technique:** Six Hats, Green Hat seed: `Volunteering you can book in 90 minutes, like a spin class`
**Movement:** White: Saturday is the only distribution day and under-50s often have children's activities or work on part of it; Red: a whole Saturday feels like too big a promise, while a 90-minute slot feels easy, so the slot became the unit; Black: fragmented slots could leave gaps, so a live shortage view nudges bookings toward empty stations and veterans anchor the long shifts; Yellow: the value is lowering the entry cost to near zero, so the path from first slot to regular slot became the centre → a fixed rota became a flexible, self-filling timetable.
**Problem it solves:** It lets busy under-50s fit volunteering around their weekends, widening who can join and keeping the Saturday rota full.
